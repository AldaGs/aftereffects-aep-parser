# The Fungus That Turned Ceylon Into a Tea Country: Script and Storyboard

**Status:** draft v2 (revised against `notes/fact_check.md`, `notes/visual_direction.md` and `notes/story_edit.md`).
**Format:** 4:50 (745 words of VO), 1920×1080, 30 fps, AE 26.5.
**The question:** Why did Ceylon give up on coffee when the rust hit, while Mexico, hit by the same fungus a century later, kept growing it?
**The answer:** In Ceylon the rust met hundreds of thousands of acres of near-identical arabica, and the British owners could afford three years without a harvest while they rebuilt on tea, so they changed the crop. Mexico's coffee belongs to about half a million families on a couple of hectares each, who couldn't wait, so they changed the tree, borrowing robusta genes. That fix costs points in the cup and holds only until the fungus adapts.
**The surprise:** Two of them. In the turn (scene 7): the fungus didn't make Ceylon a tea country; money did, because only owners who could wait three years for a harvest could switch. Near the end (scene 10): Costa Rica 95, one of the rescue varieties from episode 1, now catches rust in Costa Rica.
**Mexico's part:** Mexico is the counter-case that answers the question. Ceylon's answer (switch crops) needed capital that Mexico's smallholders don't have, so Mexico's answer is genetic, and the episode ends on the cost and the limits of that answer.
**Reused from earlier episodes:** the roya leaf with its rust-spot matte (ep. 1 S9), the world map and the Mexico map with state outlines, the Typica route line (ep. 1 S6), the ep. 1 rescue tree (shown whole as a callback, not rebuilt), the ep. 1 harvest-bar rig with corrected values.
**New colours:** tea green `#8DB33A`, Sri Lanka and its tea (season palette; it carries the island into episode 10's cinnamon). Water cyan `#4FB3C8` for the water film in S3 (season palette; this is its first use).

## Logline

A tin of Ceylon tea hides a coffee leaf with an orange spot. The island once shipped a little under a quarter as much coffee as all of Mexico grows today, until a fungus arrived in 1869. We follow one spore into one leaf, then watch the fungus clear a monoculture in under twenty years while planters rebuild on tea. Then the same fungus crosses the Atlantic and reaches Mexico, where farmers who can't afford to switch crops switch genes instead, and find out what that costs.

## Script and storyboard

Timecodes are for the master comp. Maps and charts use the episode 1 look. Leaves follow the season foliage rule: Mexico green only on Mexican plants, grey (ink outline, `#ECECEC` fill) everywhere else.

### 1. Cold open: the tin (0:00–0:20, `S1_Tin`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** White frame. A flat tea tin draws on, label CEYLON TEA in Chonburi, with a round tea-green seal on the front. It is a generic tin, not a real brand.
- **B:** The lid lifts. Dry tea leaves tip out across the table. The last thing out is a whole coffee leaf, grey, with one orange spot on it. The camera eases towards the spot.
- **C:** A small Mexico silhouette in Mexico green slides in on the right with a healthy green coffee leaf beside it, for the second half of the question.
- **D:** Mexico slides out. The camera pushes into the tin's seal until it is a centred tea-green circle. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "This tin says Ceylon, the old name for Sri Lanka, and it holds tea. A hundred and fifty years ago, a ship from Ceylon would have carried coffee. A fungus killed the island's coffee. The same fungus reached Mexico, and Mexico still grows coffee. Why did Ceylon give up when Mexico didn't?"

### 2. The coffee island (0:20–0:43, `S2_Island`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The seal shrinks into Ceylon's dot as the camera pulls out to the grey world map. A 1870 chip types on and the dot turns arabica red.
- **B:** Push in to Ceylon. A stack of 60 kg sacks builds beside the island, labelled **≈ 118 M LB, 1870†**. Next to it, a taller stack in Mexico green shows Mexico's crop for 2025/26 (**≈ 4.1 M BAGS**); the Ceylon stack reaches a little under a quarter of its height. `SOURCE: KOEHLER, *WHERE THE WILD COFFEE GROWS* (2017); USDA FAS MX2026-0026`.
- **C:** The stacks slide away. An outline of Chiapas, at the same scale, slides over the island and overlaps it on every side. Label: SRI LANKA 65,600 KM² · CHIAPAS 73,300 KM². `SOURCE: INEGI; DEPT. OF CENSUS AND STATISTICS, SRI LANKA`.
- **D:** A cinnamon stick in its object tone rolls onto the island's south-west coast, with a tea-green chip: CINNAMON, BEFORE COFFEE†. `SOURCE: DE SILVA, *A HISTORY OF SRI LANKA*`. It is the line episode 10 pays off.
- **E:** The stick rolls off. The central highlands fill with a fine grid of red dots, one per estate block, as forest-grey shapes are erased. One grey leaf icon sits on the grid. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "In 1870, Ceylon shipped about a hundred and eighteen million pounds of coffee,† a little under a quarter of what all of Mexico grows today. It came from an island smaller than the state of Chiapas. Before coffee, the island was known for its cinnamon.† British planters had cleared the highland forest and filled it with arabica, row after row."

### 3. How one spore kills a leaf (0:43–1:27, `S3_Spore`, the mechanism)
**Storyboard.** A cross-section of the leaf on white, drawn as an ink-outlined band about 1,200 × 320 px (cells as grey outlines, no fill), top surface to underside, with the stomata on the underside drawn as small paired lips. A numbered rail along the bottom has four steps, one per stage of the infection, and fills as the VO reaches each: **1 LAND IN WATER → 2 ENTER → 3 FEED → 4 BREAK OUT**. The steps are not spoken as numbers.
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The grey leaf scales up and tilts into cross-section. *Hemileia vastatrix* types on in Georgia Italic.
- **B:** **Step 1.** An orange spore (oval, 18 px) drops onto the underside. A scale bar beside it shows a hair's cross-section about three times its width†. A film of water in water cyan beads around it, and a clock icon sweeps 24–48 h†.
- **C:** **Step 2.** The spore puts out a thread that slides into a stoma.
- **D:** **Step 3.** Thin orange threads branch between the cells. A day counter runs to about 14 while the leaf's top surface stays green-grey and unmarked†.
- **E:** **Step 4.** An orange pustule pushes out through the stoma and releases a puff of spores. A counter beside the puff reads **≈ 300,000 SPORES PER SPOT†**, drawn as one spore splitting into a loose cluster of about thirty orange dots beside the stoma; the number carries the scale, not the dots. `SOURCE: APS PLANT DISEASE LESSON, COFFEE RUST`.
- **F:** Off the rail: the cross-section tilts back to a whole leaf, now dotted with spots. It drops off a branch. The branch's cherries shrink and darken as the tree draws its reserves into them (a small arrow from wood to cherry). One spore drifts off the falling leaf to the right. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "In 1869, a planter noticed orange dust on the underside of his leaves.† It was a fungus, *Hemileia vastatrix*. A spore, about a third the width of a hair,† lands under a leaf, and if the leaf stays wet for a day or two,† it sprouts. It slips in through a stoma, one of the pores the leaf breathes through, and feeds inside for about two weeks, out of sight.† Then it breaks out as an orange spot that sends hundreds of thousands of new spores onto the wind.† The leaf falls. A bare tree drains its own wood to ripen its cherries, and the next year it has less to give."

### 4. A field of copies (1:27–1:49, `S4_Grid`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The spore lands on a top-down grid of identical red tree circles, about 20 × 10 of them, in a 1,000 × 500 px block centred on white.
- **B:** A thin Typica route line (ep. 1) draws from the grid's corner back towards Yemen in a small inset, with the label ONE NARROW FAMILY. One circle carries a small grey leaf icon with a leader line: EVERY TREE THE SAME. The inset leaves before the rust starts.
- **C:** Rust spreads through the grid as an orange wave pushed by a wind arrow labelled MONSOON. Infected circles turn grey a beat after they turn orange. The rust is the only colour moving on screen. A small Ceylon outline inset fills its coffee districts orange, one by one, with a year counter: 1869 → about 1874†. `SOURCE: MCCOOK, *COFFEE IS NOT FOREVER* (2019)`.
- **D:** The grid finishes grey and compresses vertically into a thin grey bar along the bottom of the frame. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "On Ceylon, the spores found a field of near-copies: hundreds of thousands of acres† of trees from the narrow family we met in the last episode. A defence that fails on one tree fails on all of them. The monsoon carried spores from estate to estate, and within about five years the rust was in every coffee district.†"

### 5. The collapse (1:49–2:07, `S5_Collapse`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The grey bar is the chart's x-axis, 1870–1890. The red export line draws up from 1870 and runs flat. A name chip and a microscope icon over a spore: HARRY MARSHALL WARD, KEW, 1880†.
- **B:** **The document on screen.** A flat recreation of the title page of Ward's report to the Ceylon government, with a highlighter swipe on its title. Credit lower-left: `WARD, REPORT ON COFFEE LEAF DISEASE (1881), RECREATED`. The red line falls. At 1886 a marker drops to **−80 %†**, with a grey ghost of the 1870 level held across, so the drop reads as a share. Under the chart, a strip of ten land squares: at 1890, nine grey out, labelled **≈ 90 % OF COFFEE LAND ABANDONED†**. `SOURCE: KOEHLER (2017)`. *(Match cut out on the chart.)*

**VO:** "London sent a young botanist, Harry Marshall Ward, in 1880.† He worked out how the fungus spread, but he had nothing that could stop it. By 1886, exports had fallen by about eighty percent.† By 1890, planters had abandoned about nine-tenths of their coffee land.†"

### 6. Tea moves in (2:07–2:35, `S6_Tea`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The chart's axis extends to 1900. A small Ceylon inset in the top-right corner shows a tea-green dot at Loolecondera with a chip: JAMES TAYLOR, 1867, 19 ACRES†.
- **B:** A tea-green line rises from near zero in the mid-1870s, crossing the falling red line in the late 1880s, to a value label: **≈ 400,000 ACRES (1900)†**. A grey ghost of the coffee line's 1870s peak acreage stays on the same axis, so the viewer sees tea refill about the same ground. `SOURCE: WENZLHUEMER, *FROM COFFEE TO TEA CULTIVATION IN CEYLON* (2008)`.
- **C:** **The document on screen.** A flat recreation of an 1890s Lipton-style press advertisement (generic layout, no logo) with the slogan under a highlighter swipe. Credit lower-left: `1890s PRESS ADVERTISEMENT, RECREATED`.
- **D:** The advert slides away. A tea-green dot pops on Dambatenne in the inset at (1,620, 260), with a chip: LIPTON, 1890†. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "One planter had been trying something else. In 1867, a Scot named James Taylor planted tea on nineteen acres at Loolecondera.† As the coffee died, his neighbours copied him. Tea land grew from almost nothing in the mid-1870s to about four hundred thousand acres by 1900.† In 1890, a Glasgow grocer, Thomas Lipton, bought estates in the hills† and sold the tea in Britain as coming straight from the garden to the teapot."

### 7. The turn: who could afford to switch (2:35–3:06, `S7_Switch`)
**Storyboard.** A horizontal timeline rail across the middle of the frame, marked in years 0–4. Everything that switching costs is placed on it.
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The tea-green dot settles at YEAR 0, (360, 540). An uprooted coffee tree icon drops onto it, label CLEAR THE COFFEE.
- **B:** A thin tea bush grows along the rail and reaches **FIRST HARVEST ≈ YEAR 3†**. Between 0 and 3 the rail is greyed and labelled NO INCOME. `SOURCE: WENZLHUEMER (2008)`.
- **C:** Above the rail, a factory cross-section builds as a flow with arrows through the four stages of black-tea making: WITHER → ROLL → OXIDISE → DRY, a leaf icon moving through it. Heat in the dryer is drawn in ink.
- **D:** The factory goes guide grey. A line runs from it to a small LONDON chip with a pound sign; a second line runs back from a teacup on a British table, labelled DEMAND ALREADY THERE. A highlighter swipe lands on NO INCOME, the one phrase the scene is about.
- **E:** The camera pulls out; the rail and factory shrink into the Ceylon dot on the world map. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "But the fungus didn't pick tea. The owners did, and they could afford to. Switching meant ripping out the coffee and waiting about three years for a first harvest,† with nothing to sell in between. Every estate needed a factory to wither, roll, oxidise and dry the leaf. That took London money, and the market was waiting, because Britain already drank tea by the ton. Ceylon answered the rust with a new crop, paid for by people who could wait."

### 8. The rust crosses the Atlantic (3:06–3:35, `S8_Atlantic`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The Ceylon dot turns roya orange. A dotted orange route draws out of it while the camera pulls out slowly across the world map: CEYLON 1869 → INDIA → EAST AFRICA → WEST AFRICA, 1950s† → BRAZIL 1970† → CHIAPAS 1981†. Only sourced stops carry dates. `SOURCE: MCCOOK (2019); APS; INIFAP`.
- **B:** Swap to the Mexico and Central America map at the same scale. A 2012 chip. Orange fills about half of Central America's coffee area, mirrored by a half-filled bar beside the map: **≈ 53 % OF COFFEE AREA HIT, 2012/13†**. `SOURCE: ICO COFFEE MARKET REPORT, MARCH 2013`.
- **C:** The bar slides off. The ep. 1 harvest-bar rig rises beside Mexico with corrected values: **4.3 M BAGS (2012/13)** and **2.2 M BAGS (2015/16)†**, the second about half the first. `SOURCE: USDA FAS PSD; USDA FAS MEXICO COFFEE ANNUAL 2016`.
- **D:** The camera pushes in on Chiapas. *(Match cut out on the Chiapas dot.)*

**VO:** "The rust spread across Asia and Africa and took a century to cross the Atlantic. It reached Brazil in 1970,† and Chiapas, on Mexico's border with Guatemala, in 1981.† Then in 2012, after a year when the nights stayed warm enough for the spores to keep growing,† it exploded and hit about half the coffee land in Central America.† Over the next three harvests, Mexico's crop fell from about 4.3 million bags to about 2.2 million.†"

### 9. Mexico changes the tree (3:35–4:01, `S9_Tree`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The Chiapas dot grows into the corner marker of one farm plot seen from above, a square of coffee rows (Mexico-green leaves) on a slope, labelled **2 HA**. Beside it, at the same scale, three football pitches in light grey lines; the plot covers about three of them. `SOURCE: INEGI CENSO AGROPECUARIO, VIA LA JORNADA (2018)`.
- **B:** The plot shrinks into one cell of a strip of 100 cells. 84 of them fill green: **84 % OF GROWERS FARM ≤ 2 HA†**, with a chip: **≈ 500,000 FAMILIES†**. The Ceylon factory from scene 7 drops in beside the plot at the same scale and covers it entirely.
- **C:** The factory fades. The ep. 1 rescue tree appears whole, as it ended in episode 1, with a small EPISODE 1 tag: it is a callback, not rebuilt node by node. The brown robusta line glows along its length. No release years on screen.
- **D:** The camera settles on the Catimor node. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "Mexico couldn't do what Ceylon did. Its coffee is grown by about half a million families,† and most of them farm two hectares or less,† about three football pitches. Nobody builds a tea factory on three football pitches. So Mexico changed the tree instead, with the Timor crosses from our last episode, like Oro Azteca. They carry a sliver of robusta's genes, and its defence against rust."

### 10. What the borrowed genes cost (4:01–4:29, `S10_Cost`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The Catimor node drops onto a cup-score axis, 75 to 90, and becomes its tick. A marker line at **80 = SPECIALTY** draws up from the axis with a highlighter swipe. **The document on screen:** a flat recreation of the INIFAP review's title, lower-left, with a highlighter swipe on its phrase about cup quality. Credit: `INIFAP, *REV. MEX. CIENC. AGRÍC.* (2024), RECREATED`.
- **B:** A Caturra node (arabica red) drops onto the same axis. The two ticks read **CATIMOR ≈ 80** and **CATURRA ≈ 82†**, the Catimor tick sitting right on the specialty line. `SOURCE: *REVISTA AGROTECNOLÓGICA AMAZÓNICA* (2021), PERU, ONE TRIAL`. The axis fades. The rescue tree returns with **Costa Rica 95** under Catimor. An orange spot spreads across its node, and a chip types on: **NOW CATCHES RUST IN COSTA RICA†**. `SOURCE: WORLD COFFEE RESEARCH`. The camera pushes in until the orange spot is a 40 px circle. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "The borrowed genes have a price. Mexico's own farm research institute rates their cup as low.† In one tasting trial in Peru, a Catimor scored about eighty points against eighty-two for the Caturra beside it, and eighty is where specialty coffee starts.† And the protection doesn't last. Costa Rica 95, one of the rescue varieties from our last episode, now catches rust in Costa Rica.† The fungus changed faster than the tree."

### 11. Close (4:29–4:50, `S11_Close`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The orange circle is the spot on the grey coffee leaf from scene 1, back on the table beside the open tea tin, at the same position and size.
- **B:** The grey leaf is replaced by a Mexico-green Oro Azteca leaf with a thin brown vein through it (ep. 1's closing robusta thread). The tin's lid closes over the tea. The end card types on: **The Fungus That Turned Ceylon Into a Tea Country**, with a highlighter swipe on "Fungus". It holds for 2 s.

**VO:** "Ceylon changed the crop, because its owners could afford three years without a harvest, and that answer is in this tin. Mexico's half a million families couldn't wait, so they changed the tree, with a little robusta folded in. That answer holds until the rust finds a way around it."

## Notes for the build

- **Word count:** 745 words of VO at 155 wpm ≈ 4:48 (counted per scene; "4.3" counts as one word). Timecodes are allocated from each scene's word count, plus the 2 s end-card hold in S11. Retime by dragging precomps once the VO is recorded.
- **Generator:** `generators/02_ceylon_rust.py` → `json/02_ceylon_rust.json` → `jsx/02_ceylon_rust.jsx`. It imports episode 1's helpers from `schema/examples/coffee_families.py` (no separate common module yet). Simplified in v1 of the build: S3 skips the branch and cherries in beat F; S4 has no Ceylon district inset; S6 has no ghost of peak coffee acreage; the S8 coffee-area fill is dots, not a polygon. Preview: `previews/02_ceylon_rust_sheet.jpg` (one frame per 2 s).
- **Reused geometry:** the world map, the Mexico map with state outlines, the ep. 1 leaf and the rescue tree come from `coffee_families.py` via the common module. New polygons for the geo JSON: Sri Lanka (with a highland mask for S2/S4) and the Central America coffee area for S8.
- **The cross-section (S3)** is the main new asset: one leaf section drawn once, with the spore's thread and the hyphae as trim-path strokes. The rail has four steps because the infection has four stages; leaf fall is a consequence, so it sits off the rail.
- **Grid (S4):** a repeater can't vary fill per copy, so the ≈ 480 circles are generated as groups inside one shape layer. The wave is three layers: the red grid; an orange copy matted by a blob that grows from the wind origin; a grey copy matted by the same blob with a lag. The 4→5 compression is a vertical scale of all three, pivoting on y = 820.
- **Charts:** S5 and S6 share one chart rig (axis 1870–1900). The red line is the same path in both scenes; S6 extends it and adds the tea-green line.
- **Tin, advert and Ward's report (S1, S5, S6):** generic flat recreations with no real brand marks. Check the exact wording of Lipton's slogan before it appears in quotation marks; it is only highlighted on screen, not spoken in quotes.
- **Colours:** two season colours, both from the locked palette: tea green and water cyan. Leaves are grey except on Mexican plants. Roya orange marks the rust only; the tea route in S7 stays tea green and the rust gets its own route out of the Ceylon dot in S8.
- **White-frame pass:** S3's full-frame cross-section is now an outlined 1,200 × 320 px band on white (cut 2→3 updated); the 300,000-spore cloud is a small cluster; S4's grid shrinks to a 20 × 10 block and its insets take turns; S7's factory greys out before the London lines draw.
- **Episode 1 correction:** ep. 1's S9 chart says "2012 against 2016, ≈ half" with 2.3 M bags. That was a USDA forecast later revised; the actual low was about 2.2 M in 2015/16. Ep. 2 uses the corrected endpoint; ep. 1 should be patched if it is re-exported.
