# What Roasting Really Does: Script and Storyboard

**Status:** draft v2 (after fact check, visual direction and story edit, see `notes/`).
**Format:** 4:06 (605 words of VO at 155 wpm, plus a 1 s hold per scene and a 2 s end card), 1920×1080, 30 fps, AE 26.5.
**The question:** Where does the caramel in an unsweetened coffee come from?
**The answer:** From the seed's own sugar, which the roast spends. Heat drives the water out, then sugar reacts with amino acids (the Maillard reaction) and breaks down on its own (caramelisation), turning into brown pigment and smell molecules. By a medium roast almost no sugar is left: the caramel is a smell.
**The surprise:** There's almost no sugar in a roasted bean that tastes of caramel. Lands in scene 8, the turn (≈ 3:00).
**Mexico's part:** Mexico has an official standard for coffee roasted with caramelised sugar (NMX-F-173-SCFI-2020†): sugar added to the drum and burned onto the outside of the bean. It's the same chemistry run from the outside in, and it sits on supermarket shelves next to specialty bags whose caramel is made inside the bean. The cold open sets one against the other; the close answers both.
**Reused from earlier episodes:** the cream seed from the ep. 1 cherry cross-section (S1, S2). Molecule kit and caffeine blue from eps. 6–7; the S2 composition bar from the shared module.
**New colours:** none. The roast curve, drum and thermometer are ink (process heat is ink in the season palette). The dot riding the curve is the bean itself, in its roast-ramp object colour (`#F1E1BE → #E3C77A → #B9814A → #6B4226 → #2B1D16`). Aroma molecules are ink outlines; sugar is a white hexagon with an ink outline; steam and CO₂ are guide-grey dots. Caffeine appears once, in caffeine blue.

## Logline

In some Mexican roasters, sugar goes into the drum and comes out as a black glaze. Another Mexican bag, roasted with no sugar, promises caramel too. We follow one green seed through a roast: drying, the Maillard reaction, first crack and what causes it, the breakdown of chlorogenic acids, second crack. Then the turn: by the time the bean tastes of caramel, its sugar is gone. The Mexican standard for sugar-roasted coffee turns out to be the same chemistry run on the outside of the bean.

## Script and storyboard

### 1. Cold open: sugar in the drum (0:00–0:17, `S1_Drum`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** White frame. A drum roaster draws on as an ink cut-away at the left third. Dark beans tumble inside it. A stream of white sugar crystals pours in from above; they melt, and every bean takes on a glossy black shell. A small label: TOSTADO CON AZÚCAR · MÉXICO.
- **B:** A generic specialty bag slides in at the right third: VERACRUZ · TUESTE MEDIO, with a tasting-note line, CARAMELO, CACAO. Highlighter swipe under CARAMELO. A white sugar hexagon hovers over the drum with a tick; over the bag, the same hexagon outline with a cross: NO SUGAR ADDED.
- **C:** One roasted bean rolls out of the bag to centre, scales up to 520 px and fades back along the roast ramp to the cream seed. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "In some Mexican roasters, sugar goes into the drum with the coffee and comes out as a black glaze. This other Mexican bag was roasted with no sugar at all, and its label promises caramel too. Where does that caramel come from?"

### 2. What's in a green seed (0:17–0:37, `S2_Seed`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The seed splits into a cross-section. Its inside is a honeycomb of cells (shared seed-cell asset).
- **B:** The honeycomb flies out into one horizontal composition bar along the bottom (y = 860), labelled GREEN ARABICA, DRY WEIGHT, each segment to scale: CELL WALL ≈ 50 %†, OIL ≈ 15 %†, PROTEIN ≈ 10 %†, SUGAR ≈ 7 %† (white), CHLOROGENIC ACIDS ≈ 6 %†, CAFFEINE ≈ 1 %† (caffeine blue), OTHER ≈ 11 %. Source line: SOURCE: FARAH (2012); ILLY & VIANI (2005).
- **C:** The parts the roast works on lift off the bar with leader lines: SUGAR, CHLOROGENIC ACIDS, and the free amino acids inside PROTEIN (a short hatched part). A separate bar in water cyan draws above the main one: WATER ≈ 11 % OF THE SEED'S WEIGHT†, against a full-width outline for the whole seed.
- **D:** The labels fade. The bars collapse back into the seed, which shrinks to 120 px and slides to the lower left. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "A green coffee seed smells of grass and is too hard to grind. Half of it is cell wall. What the roast works on is smaller: about seven percent sugar, six percent chlorogenic acids, a little free amino acid, and, on top of all that, about eleven percent water."

### 3. The curve, and drying (0:37–0:58, `S3_Curve`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The seed is the curve's start point. A time axis draws right from it, 0 TO ≈ 14 MIN, and a y-axis up: BEAN TEMPERATURE, °C, 0 to 250. An ink curve draws from the seed as the VO runs: a dip, then a climb. The seed rides the curve as its dot. Source line: SOURCE: SCHENKER (2000), ETH ZÜRICH.
- **B:** A small ink drum icon sits in the corner with its own thermometer: DRUM ≈ 200–230 °C†.
- **C:** The curve reaches 160 °C†. The bean-dot shifts along the ramp from cream to yellow, and guide-grey steam dots leave it; the water-cyan bar from S2 appears small beside the curve and drains. Smell labels pop at their temperatures: HAY, then BREAD.
- **D:** The camera pushes into the yellow bean until one of its cells, a 640 px rounded hexagon, fills the centre. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "A roast takes about ten to fifteen minutes, and roasters steer it by the beans' temperature, which climbs along a curve. First the water has to go. Up to about a hundred and sixty degrees, the seed dries and turns yellow, and the room smells of hay, then of bread."

### 4. The Maillard reaction (0:58–1:37, `S4_Maillard`, the mechanism)
**Storyboard.** Inside the cell. No numbered rail: the reaction runs left to right across the cell as one chain, and each stage leaves its product behind on screen in guide grey as the next stage starts, so the viewer can read the whole chain at the end with only the current stage in colour. Molecules come from the shared kit: sugar a white hexagon, the amino acid an ink zigzag.
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* A sugar hexagon and an amino-acid zigzag drift together and lock. Label: SUGAR + AMINO ACID. Caption: THE SAME REACTION BROWNS TOAST.
- **B:** The joined molecule refolds (the hexagon opens into a chain): REARRANGES. Then it shatters into short two- and three-carbon fragments that skitter apart: FRAGMENTS.
- **C:** **Strecker degradation**, the longest hold in the scene. Each fragment grabs a fresh amino acid and tears off its end, which floats up as an ink molecule with a smell label: MALT (3-METHYLBUTANAL†), COOKED POTATO (METHIONAL†). The leftover pieces pair into ring molecules: PYRAZINES · ROASTED NUTS.
- **D:** The remaining fragments link into long chains that coil and fill the cell roast brown (ramp stop 4, not robusta brown): MELANOIDINS = THE BROWN. A small bean in the corner moves along the ramp from yellow to cinnamon to brown.
- **E:** The smell molecules drift out of the cell and multiply into a cloud of about 1,000 small ink dots in a loose oval about 900 × 500 px above the cell, with white all round it: ABOUT 1,000 AROMA COMPOUNDS IDENTIFIED†. All but a few dozen fade to guide grey; the few dozen stay ink: A FEW DOZEN CARRY THE SMELL†. One gets a highlighter swipe: 2-FURFURYLTHIOL. Source line: SOURCE: FLAMENT (2002); GROSCH (2001). The cloud sinks back into the brown cell. *(Match cut out on the cell.)*

**VO:** "Then the browning starts. It's the Maillard reaction, the same one that browns toast. A sugar meets an amino acid, and they lock together. The pair rearranges, and more heat shatters it into small, reactive fragments. Those fragments tear pieces off other amino acids, and the pieces fly off as smells: malt, cooked potato. That's called the Strecker degradation. What's left pairs up into pyrazines, the smell of roasted nuts, and the rest chains together into brown pigments called melanoidins. Chemists have counted about a thousand aroma compounds in roasted coffee. A few dozen carry most of the smell."

### 5. First crack (1:37–2:03, `S5_FirstCrack`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The same cell, now brown. Guide-grey steam and CO₂ dots fill it, faster and faster; the cell wall bulges outward. An ink pressure needle in the corner swings up.
- **B:** The wall splits along a jagged line. A sound-wave icon pulses: CRACK. The camera pulls out: the whole bean has split along its crease, and a ghost outline of the green bean sits behind it, showing it has swollen to about 1.5× its volume†. Source line: SOURCE: SCHENKER (2000); ILLY & VIANI (2005).
- **C:** The camera pulls out further, back to the curve. The bean-dot sits at FIRST CRACK ≈ 196 °C†, with a band showing the range 190–205 °C. *(Match cut out on the dot.)*

**VO:** "At around a hundred and ninety-six degrees, the beans start to pop. This is first crack. The water still inside has turned to steam, and the reactions have been making carbon dioxide. Both are trapped in cells whose walls have gone brittle. The pressure builds until the walls give, with a crack you can hear across the room. The bean swells to half again its size."

### 6. Development: acids into bitterness (2:03–2:32, `S6_Develop`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The dot moves on past first crack. The stretch after it fills with a hatched band: DEVELOPMENT, with a small clock counting the seconds since first crack.
- **B:** A chlorogenic acid molecule draws above the curve (two linked rings). As the dot moves, it splits: one ring goes to QUINIC ACID, the other to CAFFEIC ACID; some pieces close into new rings, LACTONES · BITTER, and, further on, PHENYLINDANES · HARSHER†.
- **C:** Bars pinned to the curve show how much chlorogenic acid is left at each stopping point: 100 % GREEN, ≈ ⅓ LEFT AT MEDIUM†, ≈ 1 IN 10 LEFT A SHADE DARKER†. Source line: SOURCE: JEŽEK ET AL., *POTRAVINARSTVO* (2019).
- **D:** Two small cups appear above the curve at the two stopping points: EARLY STOP: TASTES MORE OF THE FARM, LATE STOP: TASTES MORE OF THE ROAST. The dot moves on to about 215 °C. *(Match cut out on the dot.)*

**VO:** "After first crack, the roaster is choosing how the coffee will taste. The chlorogenic acids are breaking apart: about two thirds are gone at a medium roast, and closer to nine in ten a shade darker. Their pieces turn into bitter compounds, and harsher ones the further you go. Stop early and the coffee tastes more of where it was grown. Keep going and it tastes more of the roast."

### 7. Second crack (2:32–2:52, `S7_SecondCrack`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The dot reaches SECOND CRACK ≈ 225 °C†. A quieter, faster sound-wave icon. The bean on the ramp goes to the darkest stop and its surface turns glossy as oil dots bead on it.
- **B:** The S2 bars slide back in, each with a ghost outline of its starting length. First the water-cyan bar empties almost completely (≈ 11 % → ≈ 1–2 %†). Then a thin slice leaves the dry bar as guide-grey dots, labelled CO₂, STEAM FROM REACTIONS AND SMELLS. A total under both: ≈ 1/6 OF THE WEIGHT GONE†, with a ruler bracket showing one sixth of the original outline. Source line: SOURCE: ILLY & VIANI (2005). *(Match cut out on the bar.)*

**VO:** "Push on to about two hundred and twenty-five degrees and you reach second crack, a softer crackle, as the cell walls themselves break down and oil comes to the surface. By then the bean has lost about a sixth of its weight, most of it water, the rest gas and smell."

### 8. Where the sugar went (2:52–3:17, `S8_Sugar`, the turn)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The camera pushes into the bar's white SUGAR segment. Two bars draw next to each other on one axis: GREEN ≈ 7 % and MEDIUM ROAST, a hairline. A label: ≈ 96–98 % GONE†. Source line: SOURCE: JEŽEK ET AL., *POTRAVINARSTVO* (2019).
- **B:** Two arrows fan out of the lost sugar. One goes to a coil of melanoidin from S4: COLOUR. The other goes to a white hexagon breaking apart on its own: CARAMELISATION, ABOVE ≈ 170 °C†, with ink molecules rising from it: FURANEOL · SMELLS OF CARAMEL†.
- **C:** The rising molecules drift right and settle on the word CARAMELO on the specialty bag, which slides in at the right third with the highlighter swipe from S1. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "Here is what happened to the sugar. By a medium roast, almost all of it is gone. Some went into the brown. The rest broke down on its own, which is caramelisation, and left behind molecules that smell of caramel. So the caramel on that bag is real, but it's a smell. The sugar that made it has been spent."

### 9. Roasted with sugar (3:17–3:47, `S9_Mexico`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The bag holds at the right third; the drum cut-away from S1 returns at the left. Near the end of the roast curve (small, above the drum), white sugar pours in, melts and coats each bean in a black shell. A recreated norm page slides in, credited lower-left (NMX-F-173-SCFI-2020, MÉXICO†), with a highlighter swipe on *café tostado mezclado con azúcar caramelizada*.
- **B:** The norm page slides off. A white hexagon sits *outside* a bean outline, with an arrow wrapping round it, next to the S8 hexagon breaking down *inside* the bean: OUTSIDE IN vs INSIDE OUT.
- **C:** The drum and the hexagon pair go guide grey. A unit strip of 33 small bag outlines on one row: COFFEES TESTED BY PROFECO, 2025†. Four of them fill black: SUGAR BLENDS. Source line: SOURCE: PROFECO, *REVISTA DEL CONSUMIDOR*, JUNE 2025 (VIA EXPANSIÓN).
- **D:** The camera swings to the bag. Its own curve draws small above it and stops just after first crack, with a highlighter swipe on the stopping point: STOP EARLY. Drum and bag settle into their S1 positions. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "Mexico has an official standard for coffee roasted with caramelised sugar. Near the end of the roast, sugar goes into the drum and melts onto the beans as a black glaze. It's caramelisation on the outside of the bean instead of the inside. When Mexico's consumer agency tested thirty-three ground coffees in 2025, four were sugar blends. Mexico's specialty roasters do the opposite. They stop soon after first crack and let the bean make its own caramel."

### 10. Close (3:47–4:06, `S10_Close`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The drum and the bag, exactly as in S1's last wide frame.
- **B:** A white sugar hexagon drops into the drum with a small arrow: ADDED. Over the bag the same hexagon breaks apart into an ink aroma molecule and a brown melanoidin coil: SPENT.
- **C:** The end card types on: **What Roasting Really Does**, with a highlighter swipe under "Roasting". It holds for 2 s.

**VO:** "Roasting is a seed spending its sugar, on colour and on smell, until almost none is left. The drum on the left puts sugar back on the outside. The bag on the right got its caramel by using the sugar up."

## Notes for the build

- **One precomp per scene**, butt-cut on `Roasting`. Match-cut handoff values are defined once as constants in the generator.
- **The roast curve** is built once (its own precomp, keyed by temperature) and reused in S3, S5, S6, S7 and, small, in S9. It is ink. The dot riding it is the bean, whose fill follows the five ramp stops via an expression mapping °C to ramp position, so the bean's colour always matches the curve.
- **The mechanism (S4)** is the centrepiece. Hold the Strecker beat (C) longest. Melanoidin brown is ramp stop 4 `#6B4226`, never robusta brown; keep robusta brown out of every frame that shows the ramp.
- **The aroma cloud (S4 E)** is generated in Python, not with a repeater (a repeater can't randomise position or style per copy). The generator writes the ≈ 970 fading dots as groups in one shape layer and the ≈ 30 survivors in a second layer, with seeded random positions so rebuilds are identical.
- **The weight-loss beat (S7 B)** empties the water bar first and then takes a thin slice of the dry bar for gas and volatiles, so the picture matches the chemistry (most of the loss is water).
- **"Everything greys except X"** (S4 E): one white rectangle at 75 % opacity above the fading layer and below the survivors.
- **No brand marks.** The bag and the S9 strip are generic. No brand (Café Legal or any other) is named or shown.
- **Ep. 10 callback:** ep. 10's turn points back to S9 here (the sugar roast and café de olla are the same habit: flavour added, so the bean can be cheap). Keep S9's drum image reusable for it.
- **White-frame pass:** the S4 aroma cloud sits in a 900 × 500 px oval instead of filling the frame; spent Maillard stages go guide grey; S9 clears the norm page and greys the drum before the Profeco strip.
- **Fonts:** Chonburi-Regular, Georgia-Italic, ArialMT, Arial-BoldMT.
