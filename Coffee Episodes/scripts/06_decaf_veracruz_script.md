# How Decaf Is Made, in Veracruz: Script and Storyboard

**Status:** draft v2 (after fact check, visual direction and story edit, see `notes/`).
**Format:** 3:32 (523 words of VO at 155 wpm, plus a 1 s hold per scene and a 2 s end card), 1920×1080, 30 fps, AE 26.5.
**The question:** Why would a Colombian bean travel three thousand kilometres to Mexico to lose its caffeine, and how do you take caffeine out without taking the flavour too?
**The answer:** You soak the beans in water that already holds everything a bean can lose except caffeine. Every other compound is already as concentrated in the water as in the bean, so caffeine is the only thing that moves. Only a handful of plants do this with water alone, and one of them is in Córdoba, Veracruz.
**The surprise:** The mountain water the bags advertise matters less than what's dissolved in it. In Córdoba the extract is brewed separately for each origin, so a Colombian bean soaks in water that already tastes Colombian. Lands in scene 6, the turn (≈ 2:20).
**Mexico's part:** Mexico has one of very few water-only decaf plants in the world† (Descamex, Córdoba). Colombia's own decaf plant uses a solvent†, so a roaster who wants water and nothing else has to ship the beans out, and Córdoba's region-matched extract is what keeps their flavour. Mexico answers both halves of the question.
**Reused from earlier episodes:** the cream seed from the ep. 1 cherry cross-section (S1, S2, S8), the ep. 1 world map and Mexico map, with the red Veracruz dot where the Typica route landed (S6). The molecule kit is shared with eps. 7 and 8.
**New colours:** caffeine blue `#3B73B9` (caffeine: in the bean, in water, as crystals). Water cyan `#4FB3C8` (water, including green coffee extract), first used here if ep. 5 hasn't aired. Both are from the locked season palette. Solvents and process heat stay ink.

## Logline

A bag of Colombian decaf says it lost its caffeine in Veracruz. Benzene, solvents and CO₂ each solved the problem a different way; water was the obvious solvent and the hardest to make work, because it strips the flavour along with the caffeine. The fix is a bath already full of everything except caffeine. Only a handful of plants run it, one of them in Córdoba, where the bath is brewed from coffee of the bean's own origin. That is why the Colombian bean travels.

## Script and storyboard

Timecodes are for the master comp; retime to the recorded VO by dragging the precomps.

### 1. Cold open: the bag (0:00–0:20, `S1_Bag`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** White frame. A flat kraft-paper decaf bag draws on, centred, with a round window showing roasted beans. The front label types on: COLOMBIA · DECAF.
- **B:** The camera pushes in to the small print on the back: *Decaffeinated by the Mountain Water Process, Córdoba, Veracruz, Mexico.* A highlighter swipe runs under "Veracruz".
- **C:** A small inset map wipes in, top-right: a red dot on Colombia's coffee belt and a water-cyan dot on Córdoba, joined by a dashed arc labelled ≈ 3,000 KM†. Under it, a ruler bar of the same length: MEXICO CITY → NEW YORK ≈ 3,400 KM†. Source line: SOURCE: GREAT-CIRCLE DISTANCE.
- **D:** The inset wipes away. One roasted bean slides out of the window to frame centre, scales up to 520 px and fades back along the roast ramp to the cream green seed. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "This bag of decaf was grown in Colombia. The small print says it lost its caffeine in Córdoba, Veracruz, about three thousand kilometres away. Why would a Colombian bean travel that far to lose its caffeine, and how do you take it out without taking the flavour too?"

### 2. One percent (0:20–0:41, `S2_OnePercent`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The seed splits into a cross-section. About 100 small ink dots fill it; one of them turns caffeine blue and pulses.
- **B:** The dots fly out and line up into one horizontal composition bar along the bottom (y = 860): a blue sliver at the left end labelled CAFFEINE ≈ 1 %†, and the rest in guide grey labelled EVERYTHING YOU TASTE AFTER ROASTING. The sliver is drawn to scale. Source line: SOURCE: CEJA-NAVARRO ET AL. (2015); FARAH (2012).
- **C:** Two cups draw on above the bar, each with a vertical caffeine-blue fill gauge: REGULAR ≈ 95 MG† filled almost to the rim, DECAF ≈ 2–15 MG† filled to a thin line. Between them: US STANDARD: ≥ 97 % REMOVED†. Source line: SOURCE: USDA FOODDATA; McCUSKER ET AL. (2006).
- **D:** The cups slide off the top. The bar's grey part fades out from the right, leaving the blue sliver's left edge on the bar line. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "Caffeine is about one percent of a green arabica bean. Everything else is what you'll taste once it's roasted. In the US, the standard for decaf is that at least ninety-seven percent of that one percent goes. A decaf cup still has a few milligrams. A regular one has about a hundred."

### 3. Before water (0:41–1:12, `S3_Solvents`)
One scene, one axis: a timeline from 1900 to today. Each method is a dot on it, and the water drop at the end is the one still waiting.
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The bar thins into a 6 px timeline rail, 1900 to 2000 and on to TODAY. A dot drops at 1905: LUDWIG ROSELIUS, BREMEN. A recreated patent page slides up beside it, credited lower-left (KAISERLICHES PATENTAMT, 1905†), with a highlighter swipe on the one line that names *Benzol*. A benzene ring (ink) sits on the dot with a small ink label: NOW KNOWN TO CAUSE CANCER.
- **B:** The patent page slides off and the benzene dot goes guide grey. A dot at 1970: BY 1970, SAFER SOLVENTS†. A methylene chloride molecule (ink ball-and-stick) sits on it. A short ink thermometer stands beside it with two marks on one scale: BOILS AT 40 °C† and ROASTER ≈ 200 °C. Source line: SOURCE: MAX PLANCK SOCIETY; PUBCHEM.
- **C:** The methylene chloride mark goes guide grey. A second mark at 1970: KURT ZOSEL, MAX PLANCK INSTITUTE, PATENT†. A CO₂ molecule (ink) and a needle gauge beside it: ≈ 160 ATM† ≈ THE PRESSURE 1.6 KM UNDER THE SEA, with a small depth strip next to the gauge: an ink outline of a 4 km column, white inside, with a tick at 1.6 km. Source line: SOURCE: MAX PLANCK SOCIETY; 10 M OF SEAWATER ≈ 1 ATM.
- **D:** The three methods grey out. At the far right of the rail, TODAY, a water drop draws in water cyan with a small label: THE OBVIOUS ONE.
- **E:** The rail fades to guide grey and the drop grows to a 420 px water-cyan circle at centre. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "The first to sell decaf was a Bremen coffee merchant, Ludwig Roselius. By 1905 he had a patent: steam the beans, then rinse them in benzene, which we now know causes cancer. By 1970 safer solvents had taken over. Methylene chloride boils off at forty degrees, long before the roaster gets hot. The same year, a German chemist patented carbon dioxide, squeezed until it behaves like a liquid. The obvious solvent was the hardest to make work: water."

### 4. The trouble with water (1:12–1:26, `S4_Water`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The circle becomes a beaker seen from above, water cyan. A single cream seed drops in.
- **B:** Dots of every kind stream out of the seed: blue caffeine dots, and ink dots for sugars and acids. The seed's outline turns pale and empty. A label types on: PLAIN WATER TAKES ALMOST EVERYTHING.
- **C:** The empty seed lifts out. The camera holds on the beaker, crowded with dots of both kinds. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "Caffeine dissolves in water easily. The trouble is that almost everything else in a bean does too. Soak green coffee in plain water and it comes out with no caffeine and very little taste."

### 5. The bath that's already full (1:26–2:19, `S5_Extract`, the mechanism)
**Storyboard.** A rail draws along the top of the frame with four stops, as many as the process has: STEAM, MAKE THE EXTRACT, SOAK, DRY. A loop arrow joins SOAK to a filter column and back, because that stop repeats. The VO doesn't number them. The water-cyan tank sits in the left third; one bean is shown large in cross-section on the right, so the viewer watches molecules cross its surface.
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The beaker slides left and becomes the tank. **STEAM** lights: steam curls around a batch of seeds and their outlines grow by about 10 %.
- **B:** **MAKE THE EXTRACT** lights: a first batch soaks, and blue and ink dots pour into the tank until it is dense with both. The water runs through the filter column (an ink cylinder) and comes out with the ink dots only; the blue dots stay in the filter. Label: GREEN COFFEE EXTRACT = EVERYTHING EXCEPT CAFFEINE.
- **C:** **SOAK** lights, the heart of the scene. The tank and filter column go guide grey, so only the bean and its gauges carry colour. A fresh bean, in cross-section on the right, sits in the extract. Two gauges appear at its surface, one for a sugar (ink) and one for caffeine (blue), each with an INSIDE and an OUTSIDE bar. The sugar bars are level; its arrow draws, flickers and fades: NOTHING TO GAIN. The caffeine bars are full inside and empty outside, and a thick arrow pushes blue dots out of the bean. A rule types under the gauges: MOLECULES MOVE FROM MORE TO LESS. Hold the sugar gauge alone for 1.5 s before the caffeine gauge appears.
- **D:** The loop arrow runs: the extract goes back through the filter and into the tank, and the bean's blue dots thin out to almost none. A counter in Chonburi climbs to 99.9 %† CAFFEINE-FREE, with a bar showing the last 0.1 % as a hairline against the full bar. Source line: SOURCE: SWISS WATER; ROYAL COFFEE NY.
- **E:** **DRY** lights: the bean leaves the tank and a moisture gauge reads ≈ 10–12 %†, level with a ghost tick for where the green bean started. The tank shrinks to a 14 px water-cyan dot at centre. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "The fix is to keep the flavour from leaving at all. First the green beans are steamed, so they open. Then a first batch soaks until the water holds everything that dissolves out of coffee, caffeine included, and that water runs through a filter that catches the caffeine. Fresh beans go into what's left. Molecules drift from where there are more of them to where there are fewer. The sugars and acids are already as concentrated in the water as in the bean, so they stay put. Caffeine is the one thing the water lacks, so caffeine is the one thing that leaves. The water goes back through the filter and round again, until the beans are about ninety-nine point nine percent caffeine-free. Then they're dried back to the moisture they started with."

### 6. Córdoba (2:19–3:03, `S6_Cordoba`, the turn)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The cyan dot is Córdoba on the ep. 1 Mexico map; the red Veracruz dot from ep. 1 sits just to its east on the coast, where the Typica route landed. The camera pulls out to the Americas. A second cyan dot lights near Vancouver with a date chip: SWISS WATER, BURNABY, SINCE 1988†. Source line: SOURCE: SWISS WATER DECAFFEINATED COFFEE CO.
- **B:** The Burnaby chip leaves. A few more cyan dots flicker on the world map's edge for a moment and settle, so Córdoba reads as one of a small number: label ONE OF VERY FEW WATER-ONLY PLANTS†. A red dot in Colombia (Manizales) gets an ink solvent molecule beside it: COLOMBIA'S OWN PLANT: ETHYL ACETATE FROM SUGARCANE†. The dashed arc from the S1 inset redraws at full size from Colombia to Córdoba.
- **C:** The camera pushes back in to Córdoba. The other map dots go guide grey. Pico de Orizaba rises behind it as a single ink contour line on white (no filled silhouette), labelled 5,636 M† with a scale bar: ≈ 6.5 × CÓRDOBA'S ALTITUDE†. A water drop runs down from the peak to the dot, labelled with the bags' own words in quotes: "MOUNTAIN WATER". The drop greys out and a leader line sets a small label under it: MATTERS LESS.
- **D:** The cyan dot splits into two small tanks at the ends of their routes: one fed by the Colombian arc (COLOMBIAN EXTRACT), one fed by a short arc from the Veracruz highlands (MEXICAN EXTRACT). Highlighter swipe on ONE EXTRACT PER ORIGIN†. Source line: SOURCE: ROYAL COFFEE NY, VISIT TO DESCAMEX.
- **E:** A red dot travels the Colombian arc and drops into the Colombian tank. The camera pushes in on one blue caffeine dot leaving the bean until it fills the centre at 60 px. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "This is how Swiss Water does it, at a plant near Vancouver that has run since 1988. Only a handful of plants in the world take caffeine out with water alone, and one of them is in Córdoba, below Pico de Orizaba, Mexico's highest mountain. Colombia has its own decaf plant, but it uses a solvent made from sugarcane. A roaster who wants water and nothing else has to send the beans abroad. The bags make a lot of the mountain water. What's dissolved in it matters more. The company in Córdoba, Descamex, brews a separate extract for each origin, so a Colombian bean soaks in a bath that already tastes Colombian."

### 7. Where the caffeine goes (3:03–3:13, `S7_Crystal`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The blue dot grows facets and becomes a white crystal outlined in caffeine blue. More dots join it and the crystals pile into a small heap.
- **B:** The heap pours from the top of the frame into a plain drinks-can outline, and a second, taller can outline slides in beside it. Label: SOLD ON TO DRINKS MAKERS†. Source line: SOURCE: ROYAL COFFEE NY.
- **C:** The camera tilts to the first can's open top, an ink ellipse, which centres at 180 px. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "The caffeine isn't thrown away. Filtered out of the water, it dries into white crystals that are sold to soft-drink and energy-drink makers."

### 8. Close (3:13–3:32, `S8_Close`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The can's ring becomes the round window of the S1 bag. The camera pulls back to the whole bag, in the same position and at the same size as the first frame.
- **B:** One bean slides out and fades back to the cream seed, then opens as in S2: the ink dots are all still there and the one blue dot is gone. The seed slides back into the window. The S1 inset arc redraws once, Colombia to Córdoba.
- **C:** The end card types on: **How Decaf Is Made, in Veracruz**, with a highlighter swipe under "Veracruz". It holds for 2 s.

**VO:** "Decaf, in the end, is a bath with everything in it except caffeine. The bean keeps what the water already has and lets go of the one thing it doesn't. In Córdoba, that bath is made from coffee like the bean in it."

## Notes for the build

- **One precomp per scene**, butt-cut on `Decaf_Veracruz`. Handoff values for each match cut (table above) live once as constants in the generator.
- **The mechanism (S5) is the centrepiece**; give it the longest hold. The two gauges in SOAK are the idea of the episode: level bars mean no movement, a full bar beside an empty one means flow.
- **S3 is one axis.** Benzene, solvents and CO₂ are dots on one timeline rather than three scenes, so the viewer reaches Mexico about a minute sooner than in v1.
- **Colour:** only caffeine is ever blue, and only water is cyan. Molecules, the thermometer and the pressure gauge are ink (process heat and pressure are ink, per the season palette). The seed is ep. 1's cream `SEED`, and the roast ramp in S1/S8 uses the ep. 8 stops.
- **"Everything greys except X"** (S3 D, S6 C): one white rectangle at 75 % opacity above the group and below the kept element, not a fade on every layer.
- **Loops** (steam curls, the circulating extract) are expressions (`time % period`), not keyframes.
- **Maps** come from ep. 1's generator. Córdoba is about 100 km inland from the port of Veracruz; place it west of the existing Veracruz dot. Manizales is a new point on the world map.
- **No brand marks.** The S1 bag is generic and the S7 cans are plain outlines. Descamex and Swiss Water are named as facts, never shown as logos.
- **White-frame pass:** S3's methods grey out one by one as the next arrives and the depth strip is an outline; S5's tank and filter go grey during SOAK so the bean is the one focal element; S6 clears the Burnaby chip and greys the other dots, and Pico de Orizaba is a contour line, not a filled silhouette.
- **Fonts:** Chonburi-Regular, Georgia-Italic, ArialMT, Arial-BoldMT.

### Review notes: what changed in v2 and what was decided

- **Story edit, must fix 1 (merge S3–S5):** done. The three method scenes are one 30-odd-second timeline scene. Córdoba now arrives at about 2:20 instead of 3:16.

The story editor's line ("only a handful of plants... one of them is in Córdoba") explains why *a* water decaf goes somewhere; I added Colombia's own plant using a solvent (ethyl acetate from sugarcane†), which is why a Colombian lot that must be water-only has to leave the country. The "that's the reason it travels" line and the repeated "whatever the bean could lose" line are cut.
- **Story edit, must fix 3 (timeline order) vs. visual direction (drop the strip):** these conflicted. I followed visual direction and the coordinator: the Switzerland/Canada strip is gone, Burnaby gets a date chip, and Descamex gets no date anywhere, because the founding year (1980 or 1983) is unverified.
- **Story edit, must fix 4 / fact check (the turn rests on Royal NY):** the fact checker confirms the per-origin extract from the Royal NY report (including its Nariño example). It is still one importer's report; confirm with Descamex before the split-tank animation is built.
- **Fact check:** CO₂ is now ≈ 160 atm (Zosel's 162 bar) and ≈ 1.6 km under the sea; the VO no longer gives a number. "The first to sell" replaces "the first to manage". "Traces stayed in the coffee" is cut. "Two water-only specialists" is now "one of very few". "North" is now "away". The 97 % line is worded as a standard, not a rule.
- **Mountain water:** kept only in the bags' own words, in quotes, and the turn says it matters less. No glacier claim.
- **Visual direction:** roasted beans in the bag window; seed is cream; the S4 thermometer is ink. Ethyl acetate's thermometer mark is gone with the merge.
- **Rule 10:** the mechanism rail has four stops (the real number, with a loop for the repeated soak) and the VO doesn't number them. The close no longer starts with "So", and the episode ends on the bag, not the red cherry, so not every episode ends on the cherry.
