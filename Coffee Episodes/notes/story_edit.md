# Story edit: Season 1, episodes 2–10 (draft v1)

Reviewer: story editor. Scope: structure, question/answer, turns, Mexico, writing rules, pacing, continuity. Visual notes are in `visual_direction.md`. The scripts are not edited; every fix below is a proposal for v2.

Word counts were re-counted from the VO lines and match each header (155 wpm). Eps. 6–8 give a runtime about 12 s longer than their words imply, which means holds nobody has stated. Write them down, as eps. 3–5 do. All nine sit inside 3–5 minutes.

## Season level

**1. The palette has stopped being "one colour per idea."** The nine episodes add twelve colours to the six channel colours, and several collide:

| Collision | Hexes | Fix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Instant blue (ep. 10) and caffeine blue (eps. 6–8) | `#3F6FB5` / `#3B73B9`: the same colour to the eye | Drop instant blue. Draw powder and the factory in ink with guide-grey fill (instant as "bean taken away"). Ep. 10 then adds one colour, olla clay |
| C-price violet (ep. 9), pest violet (ep. 7), heat purple (ep. 8) | `#6A4FB3` / `#6C4F9E` / `#7A4FA0` | Keep violet for the C price only. Ep. 7 pests: ink with a hatched fill. Ep. 8 heat: ink, which is how ep. 3 already draws heat |
| Temperature has two colours | ice blue (ep. 3) and heat purple (ep. 8) | Same fix: ep. 8 draws the roast curve in ink. One idea, one colour |
| Aroma pink (ep. 8) and ferment magenta (ep. 5) | `#D65D8F` / `#B4457F` | Move aroma at least 40° of hue away from magenta and check both in one swatch |
| Tea green (ep. 2), canopy sage (ep. 4), Mexico green | `#8DB33A` / `#8FA88A` / `#2F7D5A` | Lower priority (they never share a frame), but test the three side by side |

Put the palette in the shared generator module and have it refuse a new hex that sits too close to an existing one.

**2. Facts that contradict each other across episodes.**
- Robusta's share of Mexico's crop: ep. 7 S8 says "a few percent" (≈ 3 %, UNACH 2012). Ep. 10 S6 uses USDA's 0.34 M of 3.9 M bags, which is ≈ 9 %. Use the USDA figure in both: "under a tenth of the harvest."
- Roasting weight loss: ep. 8 S7 says "about a sixth." Ep. 9 S2 says "about a fifth," and says roasting "cooked off their water." Ep. 8 S2 says water is only ≈ 11 %. Ep. 9 should say "about a sixth of their weight" and drop "water."
- "The pest that does the most damage to Mexican coffee" (ep. 7 S9) is the broca. Eps. 1 and 2 say roya halved Mexico's harvest. Say "insect."

**3. The season has a template, and a viewer bingeing will hear it.**
- Seven of the nine cold opens put a product on a white table: sack (3), bag (4), bag (6), two bags (8), latte (9), jar (10), tin (2). Five of them are coffee packaging. Keep the strongest (2, 6, 10) and rethink 4 and 8. Ep. 4 could open on the aerial itself; ep. 8 could open on the sugar pouring into the drum.
- Eight cold opens end on "So why / So what...?" and six closes start with "So...". Vary at least half.
- Five episodes have a five-step numbered rail (2 S3, 6 S7, 8 S4, 9 S3–4, 10 S5), and three of them speak "Step one... step two..." (6, 7, 8). Five has become the new three. Ep. 10's rail only reaches five by joining ROAST & GRIND. Ep. 9's rail mixes stages of the chain with a currency effect. Check that each count is real, and let at least two of them run without spoken step numbers.
- Five mechanisms start by splitting the ep. 1 cherry or seed (3, 5, 6, 7, 8). That works as a channel signature if it's deliberate. If not, two of them can start somewhere else.

**4. Repeated facts.** The 2012 roya and the 4.3 → 2.3 M-bag drop appear in eps. 1, 2 and 4, and ep. 2 S9 rebuilds ep. 1's rescue tree beat for beat. From ep. 2 on, use callbacks ("the outbreak from episode two") rather than retelling.

**5. Release order.** 1 → 2 works: ep. 2 picks up ep. 1's narrow family and Costa Rica 95 by name. 2 → 6 has no link, but ep. 6 has the best anomaly in the season and a change of register, so it is a good third release. For the rest I recommend **7, 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 10**:
- 7 right after 6 reuses the caffeine molecule and blue while they're fresh ("you just watched caffeine taken out; why is it there at all?").
- 3 then 4 cover the farm. Ep. 4's roya beat needs ep. 2 behind it.
- 5 needs 3 (the Veracruz declaration credits altitude, ep. 3's subject; ep. 3's grades are called *Lavado*, "washed"). 8 follows 5 as the next step from seed to cup.
- 10 has to come last. It reuses ep. 9's coyote truck and should call back ep. 8's sugar-roasted coffee (see ep. 10).
- Ep. 10 S8 says "the island from our last episode." In any order, that's wrong.

**6. Callbacks to plant.**
- **Ep. 2 → 10.** Ep. 2 reserves tea green "for the cinnamon in episode 10" but never mentions cinnamon. Plant one line in ep. 2 S2 (see ep. 2) so the payoff in ep. 10 is earned.
- **Ep. 3 → 5.** Ep. 3 could say that *lavado* means "washed" and point ahead to ep. 5.
- **Ep. 8 → 10.** The sugar roast and café de olla are the same habit: the flavour is added, so the bean can be cheap. That is the turn ep. 10 is missing.
- **Ep. 3 → 10.** Both episodes go to Coatepec. Ep. 10's closing bag could carry ep. 3's ESTRICTAMENTE ALTURA stencil.

**7. Unconfirmed sources under the turns.** Ep. 3's turn rests on the writer's own interpolation between two kinds of station data. Ep. 5's rests on a sampling site nobody has confirmed. Ep. 6's rests on one importer's visit report. Confirm all of them before the build, not after.

## Ep. 2: The Fungus That Turned Ceylon Into a Tea Country

737 words, 4:45. Question spoken at 0:19, genuinely unasked. The turn (S7: money chose tea, not the fungus) lands, and Mexico is the counter-case that answers the question. This is the second-strongest script.

### Must fix

1. **S8: three statistics stacked into one beat** (a disguised triplet, and the middle one isn't Mexican). Storyboard B puts "≈ 53 % OF COFFEE AREA HIT", "≈ 374,000 JOBS" (37 worker-icon rows) and the 4.3/2.3 M harvest bars into one 32-second beat. Cut the jobs figure and its icons; it's Central America and the episode doesn't use it.
2. **S8: the drop reads as one year.** "It hit about half the coffee land in Central America and cost about three hundred and seventy-four thousand jobs in one harvest. Mexico's crop fell from about 4.3 million bags to 2.3 million." The fall ran over four seasons (2012/13 to 2016/17). Rewrite:
   > "Then in 2012, after a year when the nights stayed warm enough for the spores to keep growing,† it exploded and hit about half the coffee land in Central America.† Over the next four harvests, Mexico's crop fell from about 4.3 million bags to 2.3 million.†"
3. **S11: the close says what each country did, but not why.** The answer the question asks for ("why did Ceylon give up when Mexico didn't?") is spread across S7 and S9 and never said in one place. Rewrite (≈ 50 words, +4 s):
   > "Ceylon changed the crop, because its owners could afford three years without a harvest, and that answer is in this tin. Mexico's half a million families couldn't wait, so they changed the tree, with a little robusta folded in. That answer holds until the rust finds a way around it."

### Could improve

- **S1:** "a tin from Ceylon would have held coffee." Coffee left in sacks. Use "a ship from Ceylon would have carried coffee."
- **S2, plant the ep. 10 callback:** after "smaller than the state of Chiapas," add "Before coffee, the island was known for its cinnamon.†" (Dutch-era cinnamon trade; check it.) Tea green then carries the island into ep. 10.
- **S3** has seven beats in 47 s. Drop the 1869 clipping in A; the VO's first line already gives the date, and S5 has its own document (Ward).
- **S7:** the headline "A NEW CROP, PAID FOR BY PEOPLE WHO COULD WAIT" repeats the VO's last line word for word. Keep the swipe on COULD WAIT only, or cut the headline.
- **S9** rebuilds ep. 1's rescue tree and nearly repeats ep. 1's line ("now grow across Mexico's hillsides"). Shorten: "So Mexico changed the tree instead, with the Timor crosses from our last episode, like Oro Azteca." That frees about 6 s.
- **S10:** the cup-score comparison is from Peru. A Mexican source (e.g. Cup of Excellence Mexico results by variety†) would keep Mexico load-bearing in the cost scene.

## Ep. 3: Why Mountain Coffee Tastes Different

541 words, 3:29 + 16 s holds. The idea underneath is good (the grade measures cold, not metres). But the spoken question is the generic one, the Mexican section is a read-out, and the turn rests on the writer's own arithmetic. **Weakest script of the nine.**

### Must fix

1. **S1: the spoken question is the one viewers already ask.** "So what does a mountain actually do to a coffee bean?" The header's question (why a sack carries a height) is the unasked one, and it should be spoken:
   > "This sack of Mexican coffee carries its grade as a height: one thousand four hundred metres. Buyers pay more for that number.† Why would the height of a farm set the price of a bean?"
2. **S7: the VO reads the ladder off the screen.** "Buen Lavado comes from the low farms, below six hundred metres. Prima Lavado, from the middle slopes. Then Altura, from about nine hundred metres. At the top, Estrictamente Altura..." That's 24 s of narrated table, and the boundaries are the facts the notes call least certain. Rewrite and let the screen carry the rungs:
   > "Mexico's own ladder has four rungs, from Buen Lavado on the low farms to Estrictamente Altura, from about twelve hundred metres.† Chiapas grows most of its coffee on the top two. Coatepec, in Veracruz, sits right on the top line, and that's where the metres start to mislead."
   Don't put any rung boundary on screen until NMX-F-551 is read.
3. **S8: the turn rests on an interpolation across incompatible data.** The script compares a Coatepec town station with Cenicafé farm plots and interpolates 1,650 m. Re-derive both sites from one dataset (WorldClim) before the build. If the gap changes, the VO line changes with it: "On a slope in Colombia's coffee belt, nineteen degrees sits about [X] metres higher.†" If the gap shrinks below about 200 m, the turn is gone and the episode needs a different case.
4. **S9–S10: three short scenes after the turn, two of them bolted on.** S9 repeats S8 ("Metres don't travel" / "a grade line in metres only works on its own mountains"), and S10 (warming) doesn't connect to the sack. Cut S9 and end S8 with its point:
   > "Metres don't travel. Each country draws its line for its own mountains, and what the line really marks is how long the seed had to fill."
   Then tie S10 to the grade:
   > "And that line moves. Warm the air by one degree and nineteen degrees climbs about a hundred and fifty metres up the slope.† The farm stays where it is, and so does the number stencilled on its sacks."
   Match chain: S8's isotherm hands straight to S10's line. Both are ice-blue horizontals, so the cut still works. Saves about 13 s.

### Could improve

- **S3** builds the heat budget on Colombian farms in an episode about a Mexican sack. If INIFAP or Veracruz has phenology data by altitude†, use it. If not, say "on a Colombian research farm" once so the later return to Colombia doesn't feel like a jump.
- **S7:** say once that *lavado* means "washed" (plants ep. 5).
- **Logline:** "Guatemala's SHB, Costa Rica's, and Mexico's own ladder" is three, but the scene has four countries (Colombia). It also strings three clauses together ("We climb..., watch..., and follow..."). Not VO, but it's where the triplet habit starts.
- **S5:** "The cold changes the mix, not the amount" is good. Keep it as the script's single "not X, but Y."

## Ep. 4: The Shade Forest in Your Cup

664 words, 4:17 + 15 s holds. Mexico is load-bearing (Moguel & Toledo, Lin in the Soconusco, the organic sector). But the opening answers its own question too early, the middle is a triplet of parallel scenes, and the Mexican claim is overstated.

### Must fix

1. **S1: the claim is wrong, and S1 has the first of three "not X, it's Y" lines.** "It's there because the farm had to prove that birds can live on it. From the air, that farm doesn't look like a farm at all. It looks like jungle." The certification checks the canopy, not the birds, as S6 itself says. Rewrite:
   > "This coffee from Chiapas has a bird on the bag. To earn it, the farm had to keep a forest standing over its coffee. From the air you can't see the coffee, only treetops. So why would anyone grow coffee in a forest?"
2. **S2: the question is answered at 0:19.** "Because that's where arabica comes from." This shuts the question before the episode has earned it. Rewrite the opening: "Part of the answer is where arabica comes from."
3. **S4–S6: a triplet scene structure.** The scenes are "What the canopy does / What the roots do / What the branches hold," opened by "Start with heat." / "Then the soil." / "The canopy also makes room for birds." Birds are also logically the odd one out: the header's answer calls them "work a grower would otherwise have to pay for," and no grower pays for birds. Keep S4 and S5 as the work the trees do, and recast S6 as what the buyer pays for. Rename the scenes so they aren't parallel (e.g. `S4_HotHours`, `S5_Nitrogen`, `S6_BirdCheck`), drop "house birds" from the header answer, and open S6:
   > "None of that is what the bird on the bag checks. Smithsonian researchers counted about a hundred and eighty bird species on shaded farms in Chiapas..."
4. **S7: overclaim at the centre of the Mexico case.** "So on these farms the shade trees aren't scenery. They're the fertiliser, and the reason the coffee qualifies as organic at all." Chalahuite's 35–40 kg N/ha is a fraction of what a productive farm uses; organic farms also compost pulp. It's also the second "not X, it's Y." Rewrite:
   > "An organic farm isn't allowed synthetic nitrogen. On these farms, what the chalahuite pulls out of the air is nitrogen nobody has to buy, and with composted coffee pulp it's most of what the coffee gets.†"
   Flag the "most" against a Mexican organic nutrient budget (e.g. ECOSUR or CERTIMEX).
5. **"Not X, it's Y" count.** After fixes 1 and 4, S4's "A coffee plant copes with an average. It's the hottest hours that hurt it." is the only one left. Keep that one.

### Could improve

- **S3** reads all five systems aloud (87 words) while the screen shows them. Let the axis carry the middle three:
  > "They found five kinds, and each one strips out a little more forest than the last, from rustic farms, where coffee grows straight under the native trees, to sun coffee, which keeps none."
- **S7:** the organic chart stops at three bars because Mexico is third, so it reads as a podium. Show the top five or six countries so Mexico sits in a field.
- **S9:** call back ep. 2's "Step 2, wait for water" (same leaf, same water film) and say "the 2012 outbreak from episode two" instead of re-dating it. End on something firmer than "Which way it goes depends on the shade": "In Chiapas, more layers of trees meant less rust.†"
- **S6:** the certification sentence is hard to say. "A canopy at least twelve metres tall over forty percent of the farm, with ten or more kinds of tree, on a farm that's already organic."
- **S10:** the plain answer is good. Add the Mexican half so the answer carries it: "...work that a farmer would otherwise have to buy, and on an organic farm can't."

## Ep. 5: Washed, Natural, Honey

652 words, 4:12 + 11 s holds. The cold open (a law that credits fermenting) is excellent, and the S8 turn ("The tank doesn't eat the mucilage. It sours it until it lets go.") is the best single line in the season. The problem is a 65-second detour before the mechanism starts.

### Must fix

1. **The title is a three-item list,** and S4 has to apologise for it: "So the three names in the title are a starting point, not the whole list." Retitle around the anomaly, e.g. **"Why Veracruz Lets Its Coffee Go Sour"** or **"The Sour Tank"**, and cut the apology line. (It's also the script's second "not X, Y.")
2. **S3–S4: the methods taxonomy delays the mechanism to 1:56,** and S4 is a triplet of exceptions (anaerobic, carbonic maceration, wet-hulled) plus a barista-championship fun fact. Merge both into one ≈ 25 s scene on the fruit axis, keeping the four honey grades on screen so the axis has more than three marks:
   > "There's more than one way to deal with the slime. Picture a scale: how much of the fruit stays on the seed while it dries. Natural coffee, the oldest method, dries inside the whole cherry. Honey coffee keeps some of the mucilage. Washed coffee keeps none, and that's the end Veracruz's law has in mind."
   Match chain: the WASHED seed at the NONE end hands straight to the pulper (old 4→5 cut). Saves ≈ 40 s. Anaerobic and carbonic maceration make a good Short.
3. **S11: the close shrugs instead of answering.** "What the science can say for sure is smaller: the tank's acid is what makes the slime let go." The question was "what is fermenting doing to a coffee bean?", and the episode's answer is a plain surprise. Say it:
   > "So fermenting does very little to the bean. It sours the slime around it until the slime lets go. The law gives the tank credit for the acidity in your cup. The science can only vouch for the slime."
4. **S8: the Mexico claim the turn rests on isn't confirmed.** The script says "tested that on Mexican coffee," but the sampling site isn't in hand (facts table). Read the methods before the build. If it's Veracruz, the map tag says VERACRUZ and the line gets stronger. If it isn't Mexican coffee, say "a French and Mexican team" and drop "on Mexican coffee." Also: the card says 2001 and the VO says "the late nineteen-nineties." Use "Around 2000."

### Could improve

- **S10** has five beats in 34 s (bottles, river, oxygen bubbles, pH tag, the dashed question line). Drop the oxygen bubbles or the pH tag. The 20 L figure is from Vietnam; find a Mexican traditional-mill figure† so the comparison is Mexico against Mexico.
- **S1 callback:** the declaration credits altitude too. "One is altitude, which we've covered" (if ep. 3 airs first) makes the season feel built.
- **S6:** "For about a day, the tank fills with life." Good, keep it.

## Ep. 6: How Decaf Is Made, in Veracruz (release 3)

658 words: 4:15 of VO, and the timecodes run to 4:27. The best cold open in the season, and the S7 diagram (level bars mean no movement) is the clearest mechanism of the nine. But Mexico doesn't appear until 3:16. Three method scenes stand in front of it, and the turn doesn't answer the half of the question that makes the episode unusual.

### Must fix

1. **S3–S5: a triplet of method scenes** (benzene, solvents, CO₂: 79 s, 195 words) before the method that matters. Compress them into one scene of about 35 s:
   > "The first person to manage it was a Bremen coffee merchant, Ludwig Roselius. By 1905 he had a patent: steam the beans, then rinse them in benzene,† which pulled out the caffeine and left traces of a carcinogen behind. Most decaf today uses safer solvents. Methylene chloride boils off long before the roaster gets hot.† Carbon dioxide, squeezed until it behaves like a liquid, works too.† The obvious solvent is the one nobody could make work: water."
   Córdoba then arrives around 2:30, and the match chain goes benzene ring → beaker.
2. **S8: "That's the reason it travels" doesn't follow.** An origin-matched extract is a reason to pick Descamex. The bean travels because only a handful of plants do water-only decaf, and one is in Córdoba. Rewrite the end of S8:
   > "Only a handful of plants in the world take caffeine out with water alone, and one of them is in Córdoba.† Descamex also brews a separate extract for each origin,† so a Colombian bean soaks in a bath that already tastes Colombian."
   Cut "Whatever the bean could lose, the water already holds, made from beans like it." S7 has said it, and S10 says it again.
3. **S8: timeline order and framing.** The strip places DESCAMEX, 1980s as "a third segment" after CANADA, 1988, and the VO calls it "Mexico's version." By the script's own dates (1980 or 1983), Córdoba comes before Burnaby. Order the marks Switzerland 1933 → Córdoba 1980s → Burnaby 1988, and say:
   > "Swiss Water's plant near Vancouver has run it since 1988.† Mexico's own water process has run in Córdoba since the 1980s,† below Pico de Orizaba, the country's highest mountain."
4. **The surprise rests on one importer's visit report** (Royal Coffee NY: "a solution specific to their origin and region"). Confirm it with Descamex before the split-tank animation is built. Without it, the episode has no turn.

### Could improve

- **S1:** ask the unasked half first: "Why would a Colombian bean travel three thousand kilometres to Mexico to lose its caffeine, and how do you take it out without taking the flavour too?"
- **S4:** if S3–S5 aren't merged, drop the ethyl acetate mark on the thermometer. The VO never uses it, so it's only there to make three marks.
- **S5:** "noticed that caffeine dissolves in carbon dioxide squeezed past its critical point" is hard to say. Use "found that carbon dioxide, squeezed hard enough to behave like a liquid, dissolves caffeine."
- **S7:** at 152 words it's the longest VO block in the season. It earns it, but cut "What's left is called green coffee extract: the flavour compounds without the caffeine." The label on screen already says it.

## Ep. 7: Why Does a Plant Make Caffeine?

618 words: 3:59 of VO, and the timecodes run to 4:11. The question is genuinely unasked, and the S7 turn ("The dose decides whether it's a weapon or a reward") lands. But the step-by-step mechanism is the wrong process, the poison is shown three times, and Mexico is a place name.

### Must fix

1. **S3: the mechanism doesn't move toward the answer.** The four-step biosynthesis explains how caffeine is built, not what it's for. Cut S3 to one beat (≈ 10 s):
   > "The plant builds caffeine in four steps, bolting small carbon groups onto a cousin of DNA's building blocks. One step short of caffeine, it makes theobromine, the stimulant in chocolate."
   Then make S5's nerve cell the step-by-step mechanism:
   > "Inside an insect's nerve cell, every signal leaves a messenger behind. An enzyme clears it away, and the cell goes quiet. Caffeine sits in that enzyme's mouth. The messenger piles up, the cell keeps firing, and the caterpillar stops eating."
2. **S5–S6: the poison is shown three times** (hornworm, slug, seedling roots), a disguised triplet. Cut S6. Its claim ("coffee's own seedlings") is a curiosity, not a step toward the dose idea. New chain: the S5 leaf → tilt up the stem → the S1 flower. Saves 13 s.
3. **S8: contradicts ep. 10.** "In Mexico, robusta is a small crop, a few percent of the harvest." USDA (used in ep. 10) gives ≈ 9 %. Say "under a tenth of the harvest.†"
4. **S9: contradicts eps. 1–2.** "But the pest that does the most damage to Mexican coffee eats caffeine anyway." Roya halved the harvest. Say "But the insect that does the most damage to Mexican coffee eats caffeine anyway."
5. **Mexico is a location, not part of the answer.** Chiapas is one of seven sampling sites, and S8's robusta map is a postscript. Give Mexico the human answer to the failed defence, replacing the S8 map beat:
   > "So Mexico fights the broca with something caffeine can't supply. In the late 1980s, researchers in Tapachula, a few kilometres from where it crossed, began releasing tiny African wasps that lay their eggs inside the beetle.†"
   (ECOSUR biological control with *Cephalonomia stephanoderis*, *Prorops nasuta*: confirm the dates and the institution.)

### Could improve

- **S1** sets up a cost ("out of nitrogen, which it has to pull from the soil") that is never paid off. Return to it at the end of S4: "Evolution kept arriving at the same molecule, though it costs scarce nitrogen, which suggests it pays."
- **S10:** "The bee was always meant to" puts intent into evolution. Use "The broca evolved its way around the dose. The bee never had to."
- **S9** is dense at 108 words. With S6 cut there's room to hold the antibiotic beat.

## Ep. 8: What Roasting Really Does

663 words: 4:17 of VO, and the timecodes run to 4:29. The S4 Maillard sequence is a fine mechanism, and the turn (the caramel is a smell made from sugar that's gone) is a real surprise, set up by the cold open. But the cold open never asks its question, and the turn shares its scene with a second fun fact.

### Must fix

1. **S1: no question is spoken, and the line that replaces it is a signpost.** "To see why, we need to know what roasting actually does to a seed." ("What does roasting do" is also the question every coffee channel asks.) Rewrite:
   > "Two bags of Mexican coffee, and both promise caramel. The one on the left was roasted with sugar. The one on the right never saw any. So where does its caramel come from?"
2. **S8: the turn is diluted by the 1,000-compound cloud.** The header lists it as a second surprise ("a few dozen do the work"), but it has nothing to do with sugar and sits between the reveal and its payoff. Cut beat C and its VO:
   > "Here is what happened to the sugar. By a medium roast, around ninety-seven percent of it is gone.† Some went into the brown. The rest broke down on its own, which is caramelisation, and left behind molecules that smell of caramel.† So the caramel on that bag is real, but it's a smell. The sugar that made it has been spent."
   The cloud makes a good Short.
3. **Colours: heat purple gives temperature a second colour** (ep. 3 has ice blue), and it's within a few points of eps. 7 and 9's violets. Draw the curve, drum and thermometer in ink, as ep. 3 draws heat. Aroma pink then needs moving away from ep. 5's magenta (season note 1).
4. **S9: the Mexican half leans on the script's weakest claim.** "...and with a dark roast it covers up cheaper beans." There's no source (facts table). Replace it with what the norm itself supports:
   > "It's caramelisation on the outside instead of the inside, and up to a tenth of what's sold as coffee can be sugar.†"

### Could improve

- **S7** packs three ideas (second crack, a sixth of the weight gone, the CO₂ valve). The valve ("That's what the little valve is for") is trivia. Cut the bottle beat.
- **S6:** "About two thirds are gone at a medium roast, and about eighty-five percent a little darker" switches between a fraction and a percentage in one breath. Use "two thirds... and closer to nine in ten a shade darker."
- **S9 → ep. 10:** the sugar roast is the same habit as café de olla (flavour added, bean cheap). Make sure ep. 10 calls back here.

### Could improve

## Ep. 10: Café de Olla and the Instant Nation
