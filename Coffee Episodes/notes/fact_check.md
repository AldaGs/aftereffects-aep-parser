## Episode 4: The shade forest

**Must fix**
1. S6, "On sun farms ... most of those species gone." This rests on SMBC advocacy figures (94–97 %) with no primary study behind them. Greenberg et al. 1997 (Guatemala) found shade farms had about 15 % more species and 30 % more birds than sun farms. Sun farms lose many forest and migrant species, not "most". Say: "On sun farms, studies found far fewer birds, and the forest species were the first to go."
2. S6, "at least ten kinds of tree". The SMBC norms say the backbone species may be up to 60 % of shade trees and the other 40 % must include at least 10 species, so at least 11 in all. A 12 m canopy and ≥ 40 % cover after pruning are both confirmed. Check whether the revised standard has changed these.
3. S7, "third-largest area of certified organic coffee". Confirmed only for **2021** FiBL data (Ethiopia 182,963 ha, Peru 169,078, Mexico 82,525). Put "2021" on screen, or update from the FiBL statistics portal.

## Episode 5: Washed, natural, honey

**Must fix**
1. S11 close, "What the science can say for sure ... the tank's acid is what makes the slime let go", and S8, "It sours it until it lets go". The Avallone group itself proposed **two** candidate mechanisms: an osmotic-pressure gradient as microbes eat the sugars, and pH-driven changes in the cell walls. They called the alteration "unknown". Their 2002 abstract says mucilage removal *tracks* acidification. Close: "What the science can say is smaller: the slime comes loose as the tank sours, not because anything eats it." S8: keep "What tracked the slime coming loose was the acid", and cut "It sours it until it lets go" or make it "It sours, and the slime lets go."
2. S6, "Yeasts and lactic acid bacteria ... ate about sixty percent of the simple sugars." The paper credits the 60 % to the **total microflora**, which was mostly Gram-negative bacilli (*Erwinia*, *Klebsiella*). Yeasts and LAB showed the largest *growth*. Say: "Yeasts and lactic acid bacteria multiply fastest. Together, the microbes ate about sixty percent of the simple sugars."
3. S8, "Leuconostoc can't even cut pectin." That holds for the strains in this study; other studies report pectinolytic *L. mesenteroides*. Say: "The bacterium that took over their tank, Leuconostoc, made no enzyme that cuts pectin."

The surprise and S6: "for 27 years the coffee price wasn't set by a market", and "From 1962, the price was managed by a treaty". **Wrong.** Quotas were suspended from **Dec 1972 to Oct 1980**, and again from **Feb 1986 to Oct 1987** after the Brazilian drought. **Must fix**

S3, "About seven of every ten bags it grows leave the country (2.7 M of 3.9 M)". **Wrong method.** USDA's export total includes **soluble** coffee (1.6 M bags in 2025/26), much of it made from imported beans, so total exports ÷ production isn't "the share of what it grows". 4. S6, "Before Nestlé opened a second plant, in Veracruz in 2022 ...". Nestlé also has a coffee plant in Chiapas, so Veracruz isn't the second. The 2022 opening reports give **40,000 t** of green coffee a year; 20,000 t was the 2018 plan. Say "a new plant".
