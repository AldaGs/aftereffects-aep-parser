# Coffee Episodes: Writing and Storyboard Rules

Every script in this folder follows these rules. Episode 1 (`schema/examples/coffee_families_script.md`) sets the look; this guide sets the writing, which has to be better than episode 1's.

## The channel in one paragraph

Most coffee channels cover brewing and gear and repeat the same arabica-vs-robusta facts. This one covers **the plant, the science and the money behind the cup**, with **Mexico as the recurring example**. Each episode answers one question the viewer has never thought to ask. The look is the clean, Vox-like style of episode 1, kept identical so the channel is recognisable.

## Format

| | |
| :--- | :--- |
| Length | 3–5 minutes. VO at about 155 words per minute, so 3:00 ≈ 465 words and 5:00 ≈ 775 words. State the word count and the runtime it implies at the top of each script |
| Frame | 1920×1080, 30 fps, After Effects 26.5. One precomp per scene, butt-cut on the master comp |
| Scenes | 8–14 scenes. A scene is one idea and one on-screen change that carries it |
| VO | One unseen narrator, present tense where possible, second person sparingly ("your cup") |

## Look (inherited from episode 1, do not change)

- White `#FFFFFF` background, ink `#161616`, guide grey `#E6E6E6`, highlighter yellow `#FFD84D` only for marker swipes behind key words.
- **One colour per idea, fixed for the whole channel.** Arabica red `#D9432F`, robusta brown `#7A5440`, smaller species slate `#6F8796`, Mexico green `#2F7D5A`, roya orange `#E8892B`. An episode may add **at most two** new colours, each assigned to one idea, and must say what they are (e.g. tea green for Ceylon, caffeine blue). Never reuse a channel colour for a different idea.
- **The screen stays white and clean.** Every scene of every episode sits on pure white, as in episode 1. No coloured, dark, sky, soil or water backgrounds, no full-frame fills, no scene that "turns" a colour. Big objects (a leaf, a cell, a tank, a hillside, a cross-section) are drawn as outlines or flat shapes that leave most of the frame white; a filled shape should cover no more than about a third of the frame. Accent colours go on the few elements that carry the idea, never on the space around them. At most one focal element and a few labels on screen at a time; everything else goes guide grey or leaves. Ground lines are a single ink or grey line, not a filled band.
- **Season palette (locked after review, see `notes/visual_direction.md`):** tea green `#8DB33A` (Sri Lanka and tea), ice blue `#A7CFEA` (climate temperature), canopy sage `#8FA88A` (shade trees), ferment magenta `#B4457F` (microbes and their acid), water cyan `#4FB3C8` (water), caffeine blue `#3B73B9` (caffeine), pest violet `#6C4F9E` (insect pests). Process heat, prices and instant coffee get no colour: ink, ink, and mid grey `#8C8C8C`. Coffee leaves are Mexico green only on Mexican plants, grey elsewhere. Green coffee seeds use episode 1's cream seed colour.
- Chonburi for headlines and numbers, Georgia Italic for scientific names, Arial Bold small tracked caps for labels.
- Flat cut-paper shapes with one soft shadow, leader lines ending in dots, trim-path draw-ons, a 3 % push-in per scene, 2 % grain. Easing leaves fast and lands soft. Nothing bounces.
- **Match cuts only.** Every script includes a match-cut chain table: what shape ends scene N, what it becomes in scene N+1, and the shared position/size/colour. No fades, wipes or whip pans.
- Reuse episode 1 assets where they fit (the cherry, the world map and Mexico map with state outlines, family-tree nodes, the roya leaf). Say which ones in each script.

## Vox devices to use

Use the ones the story needs, not all of them.

- **Cold open on an anomaly.** Open on a concrete, slightly wrong-looking thing (a tea plantation that used to be coffee; a decaf bag stamped "Veracruz"; a jar of instant on a table in a coffee town), then say the question out loud.
- **The document on screen.** A period map, a newspaper clipping, a trade table or a patent, recreated flat with a highlighter swipe on the one line that matters. Credit it lower-left.
- **The chart that builds while you talk.** Lines draw as the VO reaches the year; a bar drops on the word "collapsed". Every chart has a source line lower-left (`SOURCE: USDA FAS`).
- **The map with a camera move.** Pull out from a dot to show context, push in to a region to show detail.
- **The mechanism diagram.** A cross-section, a molecule, a flow with arrows. The viewer should understand *how* something happens, not only *that* it does.
- **A turn.** Somewhere past the middle, the story flips ("But that's not why Ceylon switched.") The episode's surprise lives here.
- **A callback.** End on an image that answers the cold open, and if possible on an element from an earlier episode.

## Writing rules (these are the point of this guide)

1. **Count, don't rhythm.** Never default to three items. A list has as many items as the facts have: two, four, five, or one. If a list ends up with exactly three, check that there really are three, and that you didn't stop because three sounds finished. Applies to VO, on-screen labels and storyboard beats (beats A–B–C is not a rule; use A–B, or A–E).
2. **No adjective stacks.** Never describe a thing with a string of adjectives ("sweet, bright and aromatic", "tall, elegant, low yield"). Use one concrete detail instead: a measurement, a comparison the viewer can picture, or what the thing *does*. "A tree twice the height of a door" beats "tall". If two qualities really matter, give each its own clause and its own evidence.
3. **No cards.** No grids or rows of equal boxes with an icon, a title and a one-liner. When several items are parallel, put them on a **shared axis** the viewer can compare: a map, a timeline, a scale, a chart, a cross-section, a family tree. When they're not parallel, give each its own scene beat with a visual that does something.
4. **Every number gets a picture.** A number on screen is paired with a comparison or a visual unit (bags stacked, a bar against a known reference, a share of a cup). Round honestly and say "about".
5. **Mechanism over trivia.** Each episode explains one process step by step. Trivia only appears if it moves the story.
6. **One question, one answer.** The question is spoken in the first 20 seconds. The answer is spoken plainly before the close. Everything in between earns its place by moving toward it.
7. **Mexico is load-bearing.** Mexico isn't a postscript; the Mexican part should change or deepen the answer, not just repeat it with a map.
8. **Banned phrasing:** "Here's the thing", "Let's dive in", "But here's where it gets interesting", "a rich tapestry", "not just X, it's Y" (more than once per script), "in a world where", rhetorical question chains (max one rhetorical question after the opening), "game-changer", "journey" for coffee moving around.
9. **Sentences people say.** Read every VO line aloud. Vary sentence length. Prefer verbs to nouns ("the price fell" not "a decline in the price occurred").
10. **No season formulas.** Five is not the new three: a step rail has as many steps as the process, and not every episode needs a rail. Vary cold opens (not always a product on a table), don't end every cold open on "So why…?" or start every close with "So". Don't retell the 2012 roya story outside episode 2; point back to it.
11. **Flag facts.** Mark every date, number and claim that needs checking with †, and list them at the end with the source you expect to confirm it.

## Script template

Copy this structure. Section names match episode 1's script so the build pipeline stays the same.

```
# <Episode title>: Script and Storyboard

**Status:** draft v<N>.
**Format:** <m:ss> (<word count> words of VO), 1920×1080, 30 fps, AE 26.5.
**The question:** <one sentence the viewer has never asked>
**The answer:** <one or two sentences>
**The surprise:** <what the viewer won't expect, and where it lands>
**Mexico's part:** <how Mexico changes the answer>
**Reused from earlier episodes:** <assets>
**New colours:** <hex, idea> (max two)

## Logline
## Match-cut chain        (table: cut | out of | into | shared element)
## Script and storyboard
### 1. <Scene name> (0:00–0:12, `S1_Name`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** ...
**VO:** "..."
## Notes for the build
## Facts to check (†)
```
