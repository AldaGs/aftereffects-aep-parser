# Café de Olla and the Instant Nation: Script and Storyboard

**Status:** draft v2 (revised against `notes/fact_check.md`, `notes/visual_direction.md` and `notes/story_edit.md`).
**Format:** 4:17 (658 words of VO), 1920×1080, 30 fps, AE 26.5.
**The question:** Why does a country with about two-thirds of a million hectares of coffee drink mostly instant?
**The answer:** Mexico's best coffee was grown to be exported, so the home market got the beans that didn't make export grade, and a factory process that pulls more than twice as much out of each bean, and works with cheaper robusta, made instant the cheapest cup in the country. It found an easy way in because the Mexican home cup, café de olla, already took its flavour from sugar and cinnamon rather than from the bean. What is changing now is that Mexicans are starting to buy their own export-grade coffee.
**The surprise:** Scene 6: this year Mexico is expected to import more green coffee than it exports, much of it robusta for instant. Scene 8, the callback: the cinnamon in café de olla comes from Sri Lanka, the island from episode 2, and Mexico is its biggest customer.
**Mexico's part:** This is a Mexico episode throughout. The generic answer ("instant is convenient") is true everywhere; Mexico's answer is specific: an export system that sent the good beans abroad, a Swiss factory that now says it is the country's biggest coffee buyer, and a home cup that never depended on the bean.
**Reused from earlier episodes:** the Mexico map with state outlines and the world map; the ep. 2 Sri Lanka island in tea green and its cinnamon stick (S8); the ep. 1 cherry and tree icons (the hillside in S1 and S10); the ep. 9 coyote truck (S9); the ep. 8 sugar-roasted coffee bag (S7); the ep. 3 ESTRICTAMENTE ALTURA stencil (S10); the ep. 4 Mexico City outline (S2).
**New colours:** none. Instant coffee is mid grey `#8C8C8C` with an ink outline (season palette), the clay pot is an ink outline with a sand fill `#C9A56E`, process heat is ink, water is water cyan, Sri Lanka is tea green.

## Logline

A jar of instant coffee sits on a kitchen table in Coatepec, Veracruz, with coffee trees on the hill outside. We follow Mexico's best beans out of the port, then open the factory that replaced them to see how it makes one cheap bean go twice as far. The turn is the clay pot: Mexico's home cup was already built on sugar and cinnamon, so powder had an easy way in, and the cinnamon comes from the island that lost its coffee in episode 2. It ends back at the table, where coffee from the hill has started to stay home.

## Script and storyboard

Timecodes are for the master comp.

### 1. Cold open: a jar in coffee country (0:00–0:21, `S1_Jar`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** White frame. A table line and a window frame draw on in ink, nothing more. Through the window, a hillside drawn as one ink slope line, planted with rows of ep. 1 tree icons (Mexico-green leaves, red cherries). A label wipes in: COATEPEC, VERACRUZ.
- **B:** A generic jar of instant coffee, no brand, slides onto the table: mid grey with an ink outline. A spoon taps powder into a cup.
- **C:** A chip on the hillside types on: **≈ 670,000 HA OF COFFEE†**. A chip on the jar: **≈ 6 IN 10 CUPS†**. Both get their picture in scene 2. `SOURCE: USDA FAS MX2026-0026`.
- **D:** Top-down tilt onto the jar's lid until it is a centred grey circle. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "This kitchen is in Coatepec, in Veracruz, and the hillside outside the window is planted with coffee. The coffee on the table came out of a jar. Mexico grows coffee on about two-thirds of a million hectares,† and most of what Mexicans drink is instant.† How did a coffee country end up drinking powder?"

### 2. Six cups in ten (0:21–0:35, `S2_Cups`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The lid shrinks into the first of ten cups seen from above, in a row along a horizontal axis. Six fill mid grey; four stay empty ink outlines (they fill Mexico green in scene 9): **≈ 6 IN 10 CUPS ARE INSTANT†**. `SOURCE: USDA FAS MX2026-0026 (1.82 OF 3.16 M BAGS)`.
- **B:** The row slides up. The Mexico map draws on with the coffee states filling green. Beside it, the ep. 4 outline of Mexico City at the same scale copies itself until the copies cover the same area as the coffee: **≈ 670,000 HA ≈ MEXICO CITY × 4.5†**. `SOURCE: USDA FAS MX2026-0026; INEGI`. *(Match cut out on the map.)*

**VO:** "Out of every ten cups drunk in Mexico, about six are instant.† Those hectares would cover Mexico City four and a half times over.† The coffee is here. The best of it was planted for somebody else."

### 3. Grown to leave (0:35–1:01, `S3_Export`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* Export arrows draw out of the port of Veracruz and across the Gulf, the thickest to the United States. No fraction on screen.
- **B:** **The document on screen.** A flat recreation of an export grading sheet, with a highlighter swipe on the line for beans rejected from export grade (broken or defective). Credit lower-left: `EXPORT GRADING SHEET, RECREATED`.
- **C:** A sieve icon at the port: the whole beans drop into the ship's sacks; the broken ones fall through into a small heap in instant's mid grey, labelled HOME MARKET†. `SOURCE: NOLASCO, *CAFÉ Y SOCIEDAD EN MÉXICO* (1985)`.
- **D:** The camera pushes in on the grey heap until it is one grey dot at (360, 540). *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "Mexico planted coffee to sell abroad, and it still ships much of its best arabica out, most of it to the United States.† For most of the twentieth century, the beans that made export grade went to the port, and the home market got what was left:† the broken beans and the ones with defects. That's cheap coffee, and cheap coffee is what a factory needs."

### 4. A Swiss answer to a Brazilian surplus (1:01–1:24, `S4_Nescafe`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The grey dot is the left end of a timeline. **1930:** a small Brazil inset with a mountain of grey sacks, label UNSOLD SURPLUS†, and a line to a chip reading NESTLÉ, VEVEY.
- **B:** **1938:** a grey jar icon, label NESCAFÉ LAUNCHED†. `SOURCE: NESCAFÉ COMPANY HISTORY`.
- **C:** **1949:** the line jumps to a Mexico pin, chip ARRIVES IN MEXICO† (on screen only; the VO skips it). `SOURCE: MERCA2.0`.
- **D:** **Today:** a chip, NESTLÉ SAYS: BIGGEST COFFEE BUYER IN MEXICO†, and a factory outline in ink with a grey fill on Toluca, outside Mexico City. The outline is drawn at its pre-2013 size, then again 30 % larger after that year's expansion†. `SOURCE: EL FINANCIERO (4 AUG 2013)`. *(Match cut out on the factory.)*

**VO:** "The factory came from Switzerland. In 1930, Brazil, buried in unsold coffee, asked Nestlé to find a way to keep it.† Nestlé's answer, in 1938, was Nescafé, coffee you dissolve.† Nestlé now says it is the biggest buyer of coffee in Mexico,† and it has called its plant in Toluca, outside Mexico City, its largest instant coffee factory.†"

### 5. How a bean becomes powder (1:24–2:09, `S5_Soluble`, the mechanism)
**Storyboard.** The factory outline drops its grey fill and scales up into a cross-section on white, drawn in ink outlines, that reads left to right as one flow with arrows. Each stage goes guide grey once the flow has passed it, so only the current stage carries colour. No numbered rail: the arrows carry the order. Process heat is drawn in ink, water in water cyan.
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* Beans roll into a drum and come out at the roast ramp's darkest stop (`#2B1D16`), then pass through a grinder as coarse grounds.
- **B:** A row of six tall steel columns fills with grounds. A water-cyan line enters at the most-spent column and runs through them in turn towards the freshest grounds. An ink pressure gauge rises; an ink thermometer passes 100 °C and stops at **≈ 180 °C†**, next to a ghost kettle marked 100 °C for comparison. `SOURCE: CLARKE & VITZTHUM, *COFFEE: RECENT DEVELOPMENTS* (2001)`.
- **C:** Two bars on one axis, the share of the grounds' weight that ends up dissolved: a kitchen pot **≈ 20 %†** and the columns **≈ 45 %†**, the second more than twice the first. `SOURCE: SCA BREWING CONTROL CHART; CLARKE & VITZTHUM (2001)`.
- **D:** The dark liquid passes through an evaporator and thickens.
- **E:** It splits two ways: sprayed into a tall tower of hot air, where droplets fall as grey powder, or frozen into a slab, broken into granules and dried under vacuum.
- **F:** A teaspoon of grey powder sits on a scale: **2 g**. Beside it, a pile of green beans in ep. 1's cream seed colour: **≈ 5 G GREEN†**. Beside that, the pile for a cup brewed in a pot: **≈ 10 G GREEN†**, twice the height. `SOURCE: SCA GOLDEN CUP RATIO; NESTLÉ SERVING SIZE`. The 5 g pile pours into a sack icon. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "Inside, the beans are roasted and ground, then packed into a line of tall steel columns. Water runs through, column after column, under pressure, so it can be heated far past boiling, to about a hundred and eighty degrees.† At that heat, water dissolves things a kitchen pot can't. A pot pulls about a fifth of the weight out of the grounds.† The columns pull out more than twice that.† The liquid is concentrated, then dried, either sprayed into a tower of hot air or frozen and dried under vacuum. A teaspoon of powder, about two grams, makes a cup. It took about five grams of green beans.† A cup from a pot takes about ten."

### 6. Coffee coming in (2:09–2:39, `S6_Imports`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The sack lands on the IMPORTS pan of a flat balance, and more stack beside it until there are 20 (each sack = 100,000 bags of green coffee): **≈ 2.0 M BAGS IN†**. Then the EXPORTS pan fills with 18: **≈ 1.8 M BAGS OUT†**. The balance tips towards IMPORTS. Chip: GREEN COFFEE, 2025/26 FORECAST. `SOURCE: USDA FAS MX2026-0026`.
- **B:** A route line draws to the import pan from Brazil and Vietnam on a small world inset, with a robusta-brown label: MUCH OF IT ROBUSTA†. `SOURCE: DATA MÉXICO (SECRETARÍA DE ECONOMÍA)`.
- **C:** The balance slides aside. Mexico's own robusta crop stacks up on the Mexico map's lowlands in robusta-brown sacks: **≈ 0.5 M BAGS, ≈ 1 IN 8 OF THE CROP†**, with a ghost of the previous year's smaller stack behind it. `SOURCE: USDA FAS MX2026-0026`.
- **D:** A factory outline in ink with a grey fill pops onto Veracruz: **NEW PLANT, 2022: ≈ 40,000 T A YEAR†**, beside the robusta stack converted to tonnes (≈ 30,000 t), so the viewer sees one plant can take more than the whole national robusta crop. A dated chip, no drawing: GROWER PROTESTS, 2019†. `SOURCE: NESTLÉ MÉXICO (2022); MILENIO`.
- **E:** A cup of instant appears from above in front of the plant, then empties to its ink outline. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "The factory doesn't need the best beans, or even Mexican ones. Robusta sells for about half the price of arabica† and gives a strong cup. Mexico doesn't grow enough of it, so it buys much of it abroad,† though that's changing as lowland farms plant more.† This year, Mexico is expected to import more green coffee than it exports.† In 2022 Nestlé opened a new plant in Veracruz,† after growers there protested that it would push them to plant robusta.†"

### 7. The turn: an easy way in (2:39–3:15, `S7_Olla`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The ink circle is the mouth of a clay pot seen from above; it fills sand `#C9A56E` and the camera tilts down to a side view of the pot, ink outline, on an ink flame.
- **B:** Ingredients drop in one at a time in their object tones, with labels: water (water cyan), ground coffee, a cone of **piloncillo** (unrefined cane sugar), a stick of **canela**. Steam rises in thin ink lines.
- **C:** **The document on screen.** A flat recreation of a recipe-book caption about café de olla's origins, with a highlighter swipe on the words that call the Revolution story a legend. Credit lower-left: `MÉXICO DESCONOCIDO, RECREATED`.
- **D:** One horizontal bar shows the pot's solid ingredients by weight in a common recipe: coffee, piloncillo and canela, the piloncillo segment two to three times the coffee's†. `SOURCE: MÉXICO DESCONOCIDO RECIPE`.
- **E:** The ep. 8 bag of sugar-roasted coffee slides in beside the bar, its label highlighted on *café tostado mezclado con azúcar caramelizada*. Credit: `NMX-F-173-SCFI-2020`.
- **F:** The cinnamon stick lifts out of the pot and rolls to lie flat across the middle of the frame. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "Instant had an easy way in. The Mexican home cup was already built around something other than the bean. Café de olla is ground coffee simmered in a clay pot with piloncillo, raw cane sugar, and a stick of cinnamon. Mexicans tell a story that the women who followed the armies of the Revolution brewed it for the troops.† The supermarket bags roasted with sugar, from our roasting episode, work the same way. When sugar and spice carry the flavour, the cheapest bean will do, and powder was the cheapest of all."

### 8. The cinnamon's island (3:15–3:32, `S8_Cinnamon`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The stick stretches into a route line on the world map, from Mexico City to the ep. 2 island, which fills tea green. *Cinnamomum verum* types on in Georgia Italic.
- **B:** The ep. 2 tea tin appears on the island for a beat, with the ep. 2 cinnamon chip (CINNAMON, BEFORE COFFEE) beside it. A bar beside the island shows Sri Lanka's cinnamon exports; Mexico's share fills about two-fifths of it: **≈ 38 % (2024), LARGEST BUYER†**. `SOURCE: INDEXBOX 2024; SRI LANKA EDB`. The line draws back to Mexico City. *(Match cut out on the Mexico City dot.)*

**VO:** "True cinnamon grows in Sri Lanka, the island that lost its coffee to rust in episode two. It sold cinnamon before it grew coffee, and it still does. Mexico is its biggest customer, buying about two-fifths of the cinnamon Sri Lanka sells abroad.†"

### 9. Coffee staying home (3:32–4:00, `S9_Specialty`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The dot is the first café pin on a flat Mexico City street grid with a ring road, drawn in thin guide-grey lines on white (not a real map). More pins pop on, each a small cup icon with a green origin tag.
- **B:** The ep. 9 coyote truck drives off the edge of a small Chiapas inset; a roaster icon appears on a farm in its place, with a short line from the farm to a city café.
- **C:** The ten-cup row from scene 2 returns on its axis. The four empty outline cups fill Mexico green: **≈ 1.34 M BAGS ROASTED AND GROUND, OF ≈ 3.16 M DRUNK†**. `SOURCE: USDA FAS MX2026-0026`.
- **D:** The last green cup turns into a coffee bag outline with a green origin tag. *(Match cut out.)*

**VO:** "What's new is Mexicans buying their own coffee. After the price crashes, some growers stopped selling everything to the coyote and started roasting. Cafés selling beans from a named farm in Chiapas or Veracruz have spread through the cities,† and roasted and ground coffee now makes up about two cups in five.† The jar still wins. But beans that used to go to the port are being sold down the hill."

### 10. Close (4:00–4:17, `S10_Close`)
**Storyboard**
- **A:** *(Match cut in.)* The bag lands on the Coatepec table from scene 1, next to the grey jar, in the same frame: the table line, the window, the hillside.
- **B:** The bag's stencil reads COATEPEC · ESTRICTAMENTE ALTURA (ep. 3's grade). A cherry on the hillside outside is picked off a branch, and the camera holds on the window. The end card types on: **Café de Olla and the Instant Nation**, with a highlighter swipe on "Instant". It holds for 2 s.

**VO:** "Mexico drinks instant because its best coffee was grown to leave, and because a cup sweetened with piloncillo and cinnamon never asked much of the bean. What's changing that is this bag, roasted from the hill outside the window."

## Notes for the build

- **Word count:** 658 words of VO at 155 wpm ≈ 4:15. Timecodes include the 2 s end-card hold in S10.
- **Generator:** `schema/examples/cafe_de_olla.py`, on `schema/examples/coffee_common.py` (palette, maps, cherry, sacks, bag outline, factory outline, coyote truck, gauge and thermometer, document recreation, handoffs).
- **The kitchen (S1, S10)** is a table line, a window frame and ep. 1 tree icons, built once and shared; the S10 frame is S1's frame with the bag added, so the callback reads as the same shot.
- **The factory cross-section (S5)** is the episode's main new asset. The six columns are generated as groups in one shape layer; the water front is one trim path that snakes through them. There is no step rail: the flow's arrows carry the order (style guide rule 10).
- **White-frame pass:** the 4→5 factory sheds its grey fill as it scales, so the S5 cross-section is an outline on white (cut 4→5 updated), and spent stages grey out; the S1/S10 hillside is a slope line; the S9 street grid is guide grey.
- **Colour:** no new colours. Instant (jar lid, cups, powder, factories) is mid grey `#8C8C8C` with an ink outline, which ties it to the grey heap of export rejects in S3. The pot is ink with a sand fill. S5's roasted beans sit on the roast ramp's darkest stop, so they don't meet S6's robusta brown.
- **No brands on screen:** everything drawn is generic. Nestlé and Nescafé are named in VO and on dated chips only, with no logos or brand colours.
- **Release order:** this script assumes eps. 2, 3, 4, 8 and 9 have aired before it (story edit's recommended order), because S7, S8, S9 and S10 call back to them. The VO names episode two and "our roasting episode" rather than "our last episode".
